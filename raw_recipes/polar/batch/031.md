### [Biscoff Gelato Recipe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3vvNSnfocok)

Traditional churned gelato using Biscoff spread — high sugar, low fat, small cream addition.

**Ingredients:**

- **600g** milk
- **50g** double/heavy cream
- **150g** sugar
- **50g** skimmed milk powder
- **100g** Biscoff spread
- **1.5–2g** stabilizer blend of choice

**Steps:**

1. Add milk to a saucepan. Mix stabilizers with a small portion of the sugar, then whisk into the cold milk.
2. Combine remaining sugar with the skimmed milk powder and add to the pan. (Mixing milk powder with sugar first helps it dissolve without clumping.)
3. Heat the mixture, stirring, until the Biscoff spread is fully dissolved in.
4. Once the mix reaches 60–65°C, add the cream and stir to combine.
5. Remove from heat and transfer to a container. Chill in the fridge for 3–4 hours until completely cold.
6. Give the base a final stir, then churn in an ice cream machine — Nick's took ~15–16 minutes.
7. Transfer to tubs and freeze for at least 4 hours before serving.
8. Remove from freezer 5–6 minutes before serving — gelato is served at a slightly warmer temperature than ice cream.

**Notes:**

- Gelato style: high sugar, low fat — distinct from the American-style Biscoff ice cream version Nick also made (linked in the description).
- Stabilizer is important for texture and longevity beyond 24 hours; without it the texture suffers on storage.
- If milk powder clumps when added to liquid, pre-mixing it with sugar dissolves it almost instantly.
- Biscoff is a caramelized, cinnamon-flavored cookie spread — the same biscuit found on hotel pillows and at breakfast buffets.
