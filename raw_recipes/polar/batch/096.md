### [Let's Make Ben & Jerry's Fudgy Flan](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v5Sj3ZBNqsk)

Burnt caramel ice cream base with fudge brownie chunks and a vanilla pastry cream (Crème Pâtissière) swirl, replicating Ben & Jerry's Fudgy Flan.

**Ingredients — Ice Cream Base:**
- **330g** whipping cream (35%+)
- **454g** whole milk (3.25%)
- **30g** burnt caramel (made from ~50g sugar + a touch of water, cooked dark)
- **112g** sugar
- **1.5g** guar gum
- **4g** soy lecithin (or sunflower/liquid lecithin)
- **35g** skim milk powder (SMP)

**Ingredients — Pastry Cream (Crème Pâtissière) — large batch, halve if needed:**
- **500g** whole milk (heated to ~85°C)
- **100g** cream
- **175g** sugar
- **45g** all-purpose (plain) flour
- **2** whole eggs (~60g)
- **10g** honey (or glucose / invert sugar)
- **5g** butter
- **7.5g** vanilla extract
- **2.5g** vanilla bean powder (optional)
- Pinch of salt

**Ingredients — Mix-ins:**
- Fudge brownies, chopped (store-bought or homemade — Costco two-bite brownies recommended; sugar content keeps them from freezing rock-hard)

**Steps:**

1. **Make the burnt caramel.** Place ~50g sugar in a pan with a small splash of water (just enough to wet the sugar). Heat on medium-high without stirring, swirling, or touching it. Watch closely — once the syrup thickens and starts to brown it will darken fast. Cook to a deep, dark colour (go further than you think — the dairy will mute the flavour). You need 30g of finished caramel.
2. **Build the ice cream base.** Combine the milk, cream, SMP, sugar, guar gum, and lecithin in a bowl and stir to dissolve the dry ingredients. This is a semi-cold process — no need to heat the whole base since no egg yolks are used.
3. **Incorporate the caramel.** Do not pour hot caramel directly into cold dairy — it will seize solid. Instead, take a small amount of the base, microwave it until warm, then whisk the 30g hot caramel into that warm portion until liquid. Stir the tempered caramel-dairy back into the main base. If it seizes anyway, microwave the whole mix in 5-second bursts while stirring until smooth. Aim for a homogeneous caramel dairy mix; cool it back down to fridge temperature (2–3°C).
4. **Freeze the base.** Pour into the Creami pint, freeze for at least 24 hours.
5. **Make the pastry cream.** Heat the milk and cream in a microwave or saucepan to ~85°C. In a separate bowl, whisk together sugar, flour, and eggs until a smooth pale paste. Slowly stream the hot milk-cream into the egg mixture a little at a time, whisking constantly (don't dump it all at once or it won't combine easily). Transfer back to a pan, heat on medium-high, stirring constantly until it thickens at the simmer — the flour will cause it to thicken noticeably once it approaches boiling point. Remove from heat; stir in salt, honey, vanilla extract, butter, and vanilla bean powder if using. Press cling film directly onto the surface to prevent a skin, cool to room temperature, then refrigerate.
6. **Spin the ice cream.** Process the frozen pint on Ice Cream mode.
7. **Load the pint.** Scoop out a divot, layer in chopped brownie chunks and spoonfuls of cold pastry cream. Use the Mix-In mode or fold gently to distribute.

**Notes:**
- Nick explicitly recommends making this hot process instead — heating the full base, adding the caramel while both are warm, then cooling. The cold-process approach shown is difficult and demonstrated as a proof-of-concept.
- Burnt caramel loses a lot of flavour when diluted into dairy — cook it darker than feels comfortable to compensate.
- Brownies with high sugar content stay soft in the freezer; sugar-loaded store-bought brownies (e.g. Costco two-bite) work particularly well as mix-ins.
- The pastry cream batch yields ~800g — more than enough for one pint; freeze or refrigerate the remainder for other uses.
- Pastry cream will split if frozen in the base — only ever add it as a swirl/mix-in after spinning.
- Lecithin type is flexible — soy, sunflower, or liquid lecithin all work equally here.
