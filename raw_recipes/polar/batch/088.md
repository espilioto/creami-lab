### [Irresistible Häagen Dazs Chocolate Peanut Butter Recipe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VykKwEV-82w)

Homemade dupe of the original Häagen-Dazs Chocolate Peanut Butter flavour — rich chocolate base with frozen peanut butter chunks and a xylitol-based peanut butter swirl that stays soft in the freezer.

**Ingredients:**

Base:
- **250ml** double cream (heavy cream)
- **125ml** milk
- **125g** sugar (granulated or caster)
- **100g** 70% Belgian chocolate
- **2 tbsp** dark cocoa powder
- **2 tbsp** milk powder

Peanut butter swirl:
- **50g** xylitol
- **50ml** water
- **30–40g** peanut butter (Skippy used)

Peanut butter chunks:
- Peanut butter (Skippy), dolloped as lumps — amount to taste

**Steps:**

1. Combine all base ingredients (cream, milk, sugar, chocolate, cocoa powder, milk powder) in a saucepan and gently heat, stirring until the chocolate melts and cocoa disperses fully.
2. Remove from heat. Chill in the fridge for at least 4 hours.
3. For the chunks: dollop spoonfuls of peanut butter onto a frozen plate and freeze until solid.
4. For the swirl: combine xylitol and water in a small pan. Reduce by half like a simple syrup (note: xylitol syrup won't thicken as much as cane sugar syrup). Stir in peanut butter until well combined. Store in a container; remove from fridge 20 minutes before assembly to soften.
5. Once the base is chilled, stir in the milk powder (added at this stage as a stabilizer for long-term freezer storage).
6. Layer the base into your tub, adding frozen peanut butter chunks and swirling in the peanut butter syrup as you go.
7. Freeze for at least 6–7 hours before serving.

**Notes:**

- The xylitol simple syrup is key — plain peanut butter goes rock hard in the freezer, but the xylitol syrup keeps the swirl pliable and scoopable.
- Frozen peanut butter chunks (Skippy) freeze hard but melt very quickly in the mouth — don't worry about the texture.
- Milk powder acts as a stabilizer and improves texture if storing long-term in the freezer.
- The chocolate base is essentially the same as Polar's Häagen-Dazs Belgian Chocolate recipe — use that as a reference.
- The recipe is a traditional full-fat base (double cream + sugar) — not a low-cal version.
