### [Ultra-Smooth Chocolate Soft Serve Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRo8UG3RnQ0)

Chocolate soft serve formulated for draw at -6°C: 10-12% fat, lower sugar than hard-pack, minimal guar for elasticity without gumminess.

**Ingredients:** (915 g total)
- **480 g** whole milk (3.25% fat)
- **200 g** heavy cream (33-36% fat)
- **40 g** skim milk powder
- **140 g** sugar (sucrose)
- **50 g** unsweetened cocoa powder (10% fat — use a strong-profile cocoa if possible)
- **~1 g** salt
- **~4 g** vanilla extract (1 tsp)
- **0.25 g** guar gum (0.2-0.3 g range)

**Steps:**
1. Weigh milk and cream together into a saucepan.
2. In a separate small container, combine all dry ingredients (sugar, skim milk powder, salt, cocoa powder, guar gum) and whisk together to break up clumps and distribute the guar evenly.
3. Add dry ingredients to the liquid and stir to combine, then heat on the stove to ~50-55°C — just enough to dissolve the cocoa powder. No need to go higher; guar is a cold-process stabilizer.
4. Remove from heat and let cool for a few minutes. Once below ~60°C, add vanilla extract (adding it too hot burns off the volatile aromatics).
5. Chill in the refrigerator overnight to fully hydrate the stabilizer and rest the serum phase.
6. Churn in a soft serve machine (Goumia/Gormia horizontal batch freezer used here) until the soft serve draws at the correct texture.

**Notes:**
- Soft serve recipes differ from hard-pack: target 10-12% fat and lower sugar to match soft serve draw temperature (~-6°C); too much sugar and the mix won't freeze properly in the machine.
- Horizontal batch freezer machines (like the Gormia) incorporate air significantly better than vertical-bowl home churners — a genuine advantage for soft serve.
- Guar at 0.25 g provides elasticity and shape-holding; do not exceed — more guar = gummy/stringy texture.
- Use a strong-profile (darker, more intense) cocoa powder for best flavour at this 50 g load.
- Vanilla extract goes in after the base cools below ~60°C to preserve volatile aromatics.
- Rest overnight in the fridge; the stabilizer needs time to fully hydrate before churning.
