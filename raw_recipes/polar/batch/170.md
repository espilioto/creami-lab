### [Why You Need to Try Ample Hills' Coffee Toffee Coffee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7-yCOiki3do)

A deep espresso ice cream inspired by Ample Hills Creamery (US), layered with homemade coffee toffee pieces coated in coffee-spiked milk chocolate.

**Ingredients:**

_Base:_
- **270ml** milk @ 1.8%
- **300ml** cream @ 49%
- **75g** sugar
- **40g** dextrose
- **30g** corn syrup / golden syrup
- **35g** skimmed milk powder
- **0.75g** stabiliser (LBG + guar gum combination)
- **7g** dark brown sugar
- **10g** espresso powder

_Coffee Toffee:_
- **200g** sugar
- **250g** butter
- **5g** espresso powder

_Chocolate topping (for the toffee):_
- **100g** milk chocolate
- **1 tsp** coffee extract

**Steps:**

1. Combine all base ingredients except cream in a pan. Warm gently, adding the 10g espresso powder. Heat to 65°C (or to whatever temperature your stabiliser requires).
2. Remove from heat, add the cream, then refrigerate until completely chilled.
3. Make the toffee: melt 250g butter and 200g sugar together in a pan. Add the 5g espresso powder (it will sit on top until the butter and sugar fully melt — this is normal). Cook to 125–130°C.
4. Pour toffee into a decent-sized pan (8x8 or slightly larger) lined with baking parchment. Leave to cool completely.
5. Melt the 100g milk chocolate, stir in 1 tsp coffee extract, and spread over the cooled toffee. Refrigerate until set, then transfer to the freezer.
6. Churn the chilled base as usual. While churning, remove the frozen chocolate-toffee slab and smash or chop into small pieces. Return pieces to the freezer immediately so they don't soften.
7. Layer churned ice cream and toffee pieces into a tub. Freeze for at least 3 hours before serving.

**Notes:**

- This is a traditional churned ice cream recipe — not a Ninja Creami no-churn recipe.
- Stabiliser is optional; Nick uses an LBG + guar gum combination. Can be left out entirely.
- Keep toffee pieces frozen right up until layering — they soften quickly at room temperature.
- A slightly larger than 8x8 pan works better for spreading the toffee thin enough for easy breaking.
- Inspired by the "Whirly Bird" (Witty Chicken) flavour from Ample Hills Creamery.
