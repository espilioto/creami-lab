### [The Ultimate Plant Based Lemon Meringue Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XaCA_6dwpdM)
A fully plant-based lemon meringue pie ice cream with an oat milk/vegan cream base, aquafaba marshmallow fluff meringue, and a baked graham cracker/digestive biscuit crust.

**Ingredients:**

Ice Cream Base:
- **250g** oat milk
- **450g** plant-based whipping cream (33% fat or above — Nick uses Silk dairy-free whipping cream)
- **140g** sugar
- **30g** dextrose
- **2g** stabilizer blend
- **2** large lemons (zest of both, juice optional)
- Yellow food colouring (optional — a few drops to correct the off-white colour from the oat milk/cream)

Graham Cracker / Biscuit Base:
- **200g** graham crackers (vegan-friendly; substitute digestive biscuits in the UK — both ~400g package)
- **70g** plant-based butter (melted)

Aquafaba Meringue (Vegan Marshmallow Fluff):
- **120g** aquafaba (chickpea liquid — canned chickpea brine, chilled)
- **90g** sugar (added gradually once aquafaba reaches soft peaks)
- **50g** golden syrup / corn syrup (key for silky, sticky fluff texture)
- **2g** cream of tartar
- Vanilla extract (to taste — be generous, ~1 tsp or more, to mask any chickpea flavour)

**Steps:**

1. Combine oat milk, plant-based cream, sugar, dextrose, and lemon zest from both lemons in a saucepan. Mix the stabilizer into the dry sugars first before adding to the liquid (to avoid clumping).
2. Heat on the hob, stirring continuously, until the mix reaches 70–75°C. Steam rising from the surface is a reliable visual cue if you don't have a thermometer — hold it there for a couple of minutes, agitating to prevent sticking.
3. Transfer to a bowl, cover with a lid, and refrigerate overnight to age and steep the lemon flavour.
4. For the biscuit base: crush graham crackers / digestive biscuits to fine crumbs. Melt the plant-based butter in the microwave (30–40 seconds). Combine butter with crumbs until the mixture resembles damp sand that just holds its shape when pressed. Use just enough butter — too much and it will separate in the oven.
5. Press the crumb mixture firmly into a lined baking pan (use the back of a spoon, a glass, or a rolling pin). Bake at 325°F (163°C) for about 10 minutes. Allow to cool, then break/cut into small cubes for mix-in.
6. For the aquafaba meringue: chill the chickpea liquid beforehand. Add aquafaba, cream of tartar, and vanilla extract to a stand mixer or use a hand mixer. Whip on high until soft peaks form. Then add golden syrup and gradually stream in sugar, continuing to whip until stiff, glossy, sticky peaks form. Use a generous amount of vanilla extract to mask the chickpea flavour.
7. Optionally add a few drops of yellow food colouring to the churned/chilled ice cream base to give a clean lemon-yellow appearance (plant-based cream produces an off-white/beige colour).
8. Churn the aged base in an ice cream machine (Nick uses a Lello/Musso-style machine, pre-chilled). Churn approximately 15 minutes until desired consistency.
9. Assembly: layer into pint tubs — add biscuit crumb cubes, spoon over marshmallow fluff meringue, and top with ice cream (or fold in layers). Freeze until solid.

**Notes:**
- This is a batch recipe — Nick mentions making multiple tubs; the ingredient quantities are larger than a single Ninja Creami pint.
- Stabilizer is especially important in plant-based ice creams due to the high free-water content in oat milk and plant-based creams (no casein to bind water). Nick uses a Special Ingredients stabilizer blend and ups the amount slightly compared to dairy recipes because there is no skim milk powder for body.
- Golden syrup / corn syrup in the meringue is not standard but Nick considers it essential — without it the fluff lacks the silky, sticky marshmallow texture and turns into a plain Swiss meringue.
- Aquafaba quality varies significantly by brand; canned chickpea liquid that looks clear and "egg-white-like" whips much better than a murky, thick one.
- Lemon zest is added directly to the warm mix and the base is aged overnight with the zest in it, which extracts the lemon oils fully into the cream.
- Optional: a small amount of lemon extract can be added for an additional flavour boost without adding more zest.
- Plant-based ice cream will not be as white as a dairy base; food colouring is cosmetic only but Nick prefers a yellow tint to signal lemon flavour visually.
- If not going fully plant-based, Swiss meringue (egg whites + sugar cooked to 60°C then whipped) can substitute for the aquafaba fluff.
