### [Key Lime Pie Ice Cream (Better Than Häagen-Dazs)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YVJ7syvqwmc)
Zesty key lime ice cream churned with buttery baked graham cracker crumble pieces and a Swiss meringue swirl — the meringue element Häagen-Dazs missed.

**Ingredients — Ice Cream Base** (yields ~1 litre):
- **415g** whole milk or 2% milk
- **370g** whipping cream (33%+ fat; UK: double cream)
- **130g** allulose (listed as "sugar" in description; spoken as allulose in video)
- **30g** corn syrup or glucose syrup (not golden syrup for base)
- **60g** skim milk powder (SMP)
- **2g** stabilizer (optional)
- **5–7g** key lime extract (LorAnn or similar — linked in description)

**Ingredients — Graham Cracker Crumble** (for ~1 litre):
- **100g** graham cracker crumbs
- **50g** butter, melted
- **20g** corn syrup, glucose syrup, or golden syrup

**Ingredients — Swiss Meringue Swirl**:
- **150g** egg whites (storebought acceptable)
- **150g** sugar (1:1 ratio by weight to egg whites)
- **1/3 tsp** cream of tartar (stabilizer for egg whites)
- Splash of vanilla extract (added after whipping, once cooled)

**Steps:**
1. Combine milk, cream, allulose, corn syrup, and SMP in a saucepan. Mix the stabilizer into the SMP first, then stir into the liquid.
2. If using stabilizer, heat to 80–85°C to activate. Without stabilizer, heat to 50–60°C until fully dissolved and homogenised.
3. Let the base cool slightly (about 10°C below peak temp), then add key lime extract. Extract can separate on top if added too hot — it will incorporate once fully cooled.
4. Chill base in fridge overnight to let the lime flavour develop.
5. For the graham cracker crumble: combine crumbs, melted butter, and corn/glucose/golden syrup. Press flat on a parchment-lined baking tray. Bake at 190°C (375°F) for 5–8 minutes until set — do not burn. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate until firm. Chop into ~1cm cubes. The syrup is essential — without it the crumble falls apart in the ice cream.
6. For the Swiss meringue: combine egg whites, sugar, and cream of tartar in a heatproof metal bowl. Set over a pot of simmering water (bain-marie). Stir constantly with a spatula until sugar fully dissolves and no grittiness remains — scrape the bowl sides to prevent crunchy sugar bits. Remove from heat.
7. Whisk with a stand mixer (strongly recommended over hand-whisking) until stiff peaks form and the mixture cools to near room temperature. Fold in vanilla extract once cooled.
8. Churn the chilled base in an ice cream machine. Towards the end of the churn, add the graham cracker crumble pieces.
9. Layer into the container: alternate scoops of churned ice cream with spoonfuls of Swiss meringue swirl throughout. Fill the whole tub this way.
10. Freeze for several hours until hardened before scooping.

**Notes:**
- Nick filmed a double-batch; the ingredient amounts above are the single-batch quantities he states for ~1 litre.
- Allulose is the actual sweetener used (spoken clearly in the VTT). The description labels it "Sugar" generically.
- Corn syrup or glucose syrup preferred in the base; golden syrup explicitly not recommended for the base (but works well in the crumble where it brings out caramel notes).
- Swiss meringue (egg whites warmed with sugar over bain-marie, then whipped) gives a marshmallow-fluff texture that holds in frozen ice cream — the same technique used in Ben & Jerry's Fish Food.
- Storebought carton egg whites work fine for Swiss meringue despite common claims otherwise — just use cream of tartar as a stabiliser.
- Key lime extract can be strong; start at 5g and adjust. LorAnn-style oils are very concentrated.
- Add extract once base cools slightly below peak temp to prevent oil separation on the surface (will reincorporate as it cools further).
- Chill base overnight for best lime flavour development.
- The corn/glucose syrup in the crumble creates a chewier, cohesive texture that holds together in the ice cream; more butter causes leeching rather than binding.
