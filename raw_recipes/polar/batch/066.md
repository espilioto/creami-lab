### [Fixing VIRAL Ninja Creami recipes using SCIENCE - Peach](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xL8K8vMLKog)

Taking the viral "1 ingredient canned peach ice cream" TikTok recipe and fixing it with proper ice cream science: adding cream for fat/solids, allulose for FPD and sweetness, inulin for bulk solids, and xanthan gum to bind free water.

**Ingredients:**

- **1 whole can** sliced peaches in juice (Del Monte brand used — sucralose-sweetened, 8g sugar/167ml serving)
- **100g** whipping cream (33–36% fat; double cream also works)
- **70g** allulose (or xylitol, but use less)
- **25g** inulin
- **1g** xanthan gum

**Steps:**

1. Open the can of peaches and pour the entire contents (fruit and syrup) into a blender.
2. Add 100g whipping cream, 70g allulose, 25g inulin, and 1g xanthan gum.
3. Blend for 1–2 minutes until completely smooth and the peaches are fully liquefied.
4. Check for graininess by running a spoon along the edge of the container — allulose should dissolve without heating in this context.
5. Pour into a Ninja Creami pint and freeze for at least 24 hours until fully hardened.
6. Process on Ice Cream (or Gelato — identical settings). Re-spin once if needed due to low solids content.

**Notes:**

- Yield is approximately 600g of base, filling the pint.
- Two spins may be required — solids are still on the lower end for optimal ice cream texture.
- Use xanthan gum (not guar gum) as guar gum performs poorly in acidic environments; peaches are mildly acidic.
- Del Monte sucralose-sweetened cans preferred (8g sugar/serving vs. 22g in sugar-sweetened varieties) to reduce added sugar load.
- The viral "can of peaches only" original produces enormous visible ice crystals due to insufficient solids — that version is essentially frozen fruit, not ice cream or sorbet.
- Inulin is chicory root fiber; also sold under that name. Any brand works — they are all the same ingredient.
- Nick notes this fat content is technically gelato-level, but without traditional sugar — allulose provides FPD (freezing point depression) instead.
