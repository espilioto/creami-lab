### [Raspberry & Cream Gelato with Homemade Chocolate Fudge Pieces](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i2OmigGCEd4)
- **460g** whole milk (can substitute 2% / semi-skimmed)
- **70g** skim milk powder
- **172g** sugar
- **10g** dextrose, glucose syrup, corn syrup, or honey (one of these)
- **2–3g** vanilla extract
- **100g** homemade chocolate fudge (see fudge recipe: https://youtu.be/4gs4oOrHYbA)
- A couple of tablespoons of water (for cooking the raspberries)

**Steps:**

1. Place raspberries in a pan with the sugar and a couple of tablespoons of water. Heat over medium, stirring and breaking the berries down, until the mixture comes to a light boil and the sugar is fully dissolved — about 5–10 minutes.
2. Remove from heat. Transfer the hot raspberry syrup to a food processor or blender. Pulse on low for 10–20 seconds, then run on high for a further 10–15 seconds until smooth.
3. In a separate pan, combine the milk, cream, skim milk powder, and dextrose/glucose/corn syrup/honey. Place on the hob over gentle heat.
4. Pour the hot raspberry syrup into the milk and cream mixture. Stir well to combine — the mixture will turn a soft pink.
5. Return the combined base to the hob and heat gently to 50–60°C (until steaming), ensuring all fats, sugars, and solids are fully emulsified. This takes only a couple of minutes.
6. Stir in the vanilla extract (2–3g). Transfer to a container.
7. Cool, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours to fully chill and allow flavours to bond.
8. Once fully chilled, strain the base through a fine-mesh strainer to remove raspberry seeds and any solids.
9. Churn in a pre-chilled ice cream machine.
10. While the base is churning, cut 100g of chocolate fudge — use a cheese grater (fine side) for smaller shards and a knife for larger chunks. Work quickly as the fudge melts in your hands. Refrigerate the chopped fudge for 5–10 minutes to firm back up.
11. At the end of the churning phase, add all the fudge pieces into the machine. Churn briefly to fold in.
12. Dish up and serve, or pack into a container and freeze.

**Notes:**

- Raspberries are acidic and relatively low in sugar — not much balancing needed, but the sugar quantity is still important for texture and sweetness.
- Can be made sugar-free: substitute sugar with allulose 1:1, or xylitol at ~70% of the sugar weight (reduce by ~30%).
- Adding a splash of water to the raspberries at the start helps draw out the juices and dissolve the sugar faster.
- No stabiliser is used in this recipe — no need to heat to any specific pasteurisation temperature; just bring to steaming (50–60°C) to emulsify.
- Adding the raspberry syrup to the warm (not yet fully hot) base is fine — the hot syrup brings the whole mixture up to temperature together.
- Optional: add a small amount of red food colouring to boost the colour — only a drop or two needed. Nick keeps it natural here.
- The fudge is specifically formulated to stay soft (not rock-hard) when frozen — it melts quickly at room temperature, so work fast and chill before adding.
- Chop fudge using both a fine cheese grater (for smaller pieces) and a knife (for larger chunks) to get a mix of textures throughout.
- Fudge recipe (separate video): https://youtu.be/4gs4oOrHYbA — Nick makes large batches (~1.5kg) and keeps it in the freezer.
