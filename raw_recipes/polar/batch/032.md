### [Black Forest Ice Cream using an Ice Cream Calculator](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PrpvcOiU7Qw)

Sweet cream ice cream base built with Scoopulator, finished with chopped dark chocolate and a canned Bing cherry swirl.

> **Batch recipe** — yields approximately 2+ pints.

---

**Ingredients — Ice Cream Base:**

- **634g** whipping cream (33% fat; US: heavy cream, UK: double cream)
- **634g** whole milk (3.2–3.25% fat)
- **135g** allulose (powdered or granulated — no difference)
- **25g** glucose syrup (plain corn syrup; not high-fructose corn syrup)
- **70g** skim milk powder
- **1.9g** stabilizer (any standard ice cream stabilizer blend)

**Ingredients — Cherry Swirl:**

- **1 can** Bing cherries in syrup (400ml can; ~42g sugar per 167ml)
- **35g** glucose syrup (plain corn syrup)
- **10g** sugar

**Ingredients — Mix-In:**

- Dark chocolate (amount to taste — chopped finely before adding)

---

**Steps:**

1. Combine milk, cream, glucose syrup, and skim milk powder in a saucepan.
2. In a separate small bowl, mix the stabilizer into the allulose powder, then stir the allulose-stabilizer blend into the base. Mixing stabilizer with dry sugar first prevents clumping.
3. Heat the base to 85°C (185°F), stirring throughout — takes about 5–10 minutes depending on heat level.
4. Cool the base in the refrigerator and age overnight. This builds milk flavour and allows the stabilizer to fully distribute through the mix.
5. For the cherry swirl: pour the entire can of Bing cherries (cherries + syrup) into a saucepan. Heat on a gentle flame to 85–90°C and hold for 5–10 minutes, reducing the weight by 30–40%.
6. Let the reduced cherries cool for about 30 minutes, then blend smooth (blender, stick blender, or food processor).
7. Add 35g glucose syrup and 10g sugar to the blended cherries, stir together, then return to heat and bring back to 85°C for 2 minutes. Cool and refrigerate overnight to thicken and develop flavour.
8. Before churning, place the chocolate in the freezer — cold chocolate chops cleanly with less static fly-off.
9. Remove the aged base from the fridge, stir well, then churn in your ice cream machine.
10. While the base is churning, finely chop the chocolate.
11. Load churned ice cream into tubs, layering in chopped chocolate and spooning the cherry swirl through the ice cream as you go.
12. Freeze until firm.

---

**Notes:**

- Allulose has ~70% the sweetness of sucrose and a high freezing point depression effect (FPD), which means you can use less of it than sugar — this leaves total solids lower than ideal. Nick targets ~40% solids; this recipe comes in slightly below at ~36–37% due to the allulose. The stabilizer compensates by increasing viscosity and creating the perception of higher solids.
- Glucose syrup (corn syrup) is not very sweet on its own (~40 DE) and contributes body and ice crystal control in both the base and the cherry swirl — it slows ice crystal growth so the swirl doesn't freeze rock-hard.
- The sugar in the cherry swirl adjusts sweetness and provides additional FPD to keep the swirl soft.
- Heating the base to 85°C is required specifically because of the stabilizer; without stabilizer, that temperature isn't strictly necessary but still improves results.
- The Scoopulator target used was "general ice cream" profile, aimed at 14–15% fat. Scoopability target was -4°C serving point (mid-range of the scoopability dial).
- Nick notes the natural canned-cherry swirl has a milder flavour compared to commercial cherry syrups (e.g., Amarena) — you can thicken it further with a small amount of guar or xanthan gum if a thicker swirl is preferred.
- The chocolate is added as a frozen chopped chunk mix-in, not stirred in during churning, so it does not create a stracciatella effect — the pieces stay distinct.
