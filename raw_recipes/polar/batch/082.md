### [Alton Brown's Serious Vanilla Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ppesx3LOfC4)

Nick tests Alton Brown's egg-free vanilla ice cream that uses liquid pectin as the stabilizer, analyzes the numbers, and gives his honest verdict.

**Ingredients:**

- **100g** sugar (for dairy mixture)
- **50g** sugar (for pectin activation)
- **60g** water
- **15g** liquid pectin
- **480g** half and half (10% fat — North American; outside NA: blend single cream + water to reach 10% fat)
- **245g** heavy cream (34–36% fat)
- **40g** vanilla bean paste
- **0.1 tsp** sea salt (pinch; do not use table salt)

**Steps:**

1. Combine 50g sugar and 60g water in a small saucepan. Bring to a boil.
2. Add 15g liquid pectin. Return to a rolling boil for exactly 1 minute, then remove from heat.
3. Cool pectin mixture at room temperature for 25 minutes, until it reaches approximately 89°F (32°C). Gently swirl the pan occasionally to prevent a sticky layer forming on the sides.
4. Meanwhile, combine the remaining 100g sugar, the half and half, the heavy cream, and the sea salt in a separate saucepan. Heat to 185°F (85°C), then remove from heat.
5. Cover with a lid (or foil) and steep for 20 minutes. When removing the cover, avoid dripping condensation back into the mixture.
6. Whisk the cooled pectin mixture thoroughly, then pour it into the dairy mixture. Stir to combine.
7. Add 40g vanilla bean paste. Stir well.
8. Cool the combined mixture to room temperature using an ice bath, stirring continuously.
9. Transfer to an airtight container and chill in the fridge for 6–24 hours (Nick does a full 24 hours).
10. Churn in an ice cream maker per machine instructions.

**Notes:**

- Outside North America, "half and half" (10% fat) can be approximated by diluting single cream with water to reach the target fat percentage. Same approach applies to the heavy cream if using higher-fat double cream (50%): add water to bring it back to ~36% fat.
- Nick's critique: the recipe is low in total solids (~31–32%), which results in a serving temperature of around -10°C — too cold to scoop straight from the freezer. Needs 5–10 minutes tempering before serving.
- The missing fix: skim milk powder (SMP / low-fat milk powder / zero-fat milk powder) would raise solids, lower serving temperature, and improve texture. The lactose in SMP provides PAC (anti-freezing power) and brings serving temperature to a better range.
- The pectin activation step (boiling with 50g sugar + water) is essential — sugar is required to activate pectin; water prevents scorching. The 185°F dairy heating denatures proteins and integrates the pectin. The overnight chill does the real structural work; the 20-minute steep is largely incidental.
- Pectin is an unusual stabilizer for ice cream but works decently. Alton Brown uses it instead of eggs to achieve a smooth, creamy texture without a custard base.
- Nick rates it as a good, accessible recipe — easy to make, great flavor — but not yet "serious" without the solids boost from SMP.
