### [Banoffee Ice Cream LOVERS Rejoice!](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRASaquHb7Q)

Banoffee pie ice cream made without actual banana — roasted banana flesh is steeped in the base overnight to extract deep flavor, then removed before churning. Made for both traditional ice cream machine and Ninja Creami.

**Note: Batch recipe** — makes enough for one traditional churn + one Ninja Creami pint (roughly 2 pints total).

**Ingredients — Ice Cream Base:**
- **537g** milk (2%)
- **550g** whipping cream (33% fat)
- **230g** sugar
- **25g** corn syrup (or golden syrup / inverted sugar)
- **55g** skim milk powder
- **2g** ice cream stabilizer (Special Ingredients brand used; any cold- or hot-process stabilizer works)
- **3 medium bananas** (for steeping only — removed before churning)

**Ingredients — Graham Cracker Crust:**
- **200g** graham cracker crumbs (or digestive biscuits / hobnobs)
- **80g** butter, melted
- **20g** sugar

**Ingredients — Assembly:**
- Dulce de leche (real South American-style preferred — not Eagle Brand canned)

**Steps:**

1. Preheat oven to 350F (175C). Place whole unpeeled bananas on a lined baking sheet. Roast 10–15 minutes until skins are basically black. This draws sugars to the surface and develops deep banana flavor via the Maillard reaction.
2. While bananas roast, combine milk, cream, sugar (reserving 10g to mix with stabilizer), corn syrup, and skim milk powder in a saucepan. Mix the stabilizer with the reserved 10g sugar, then add to the pan. Heat on the hob to 85C (185F), stirring continuously.
3. Pour the hot ice cream base into a large bowl. Squeeze the roasted banana flesh out of the skins (discard skins) and add the flesh directly to the hot base. Steep overnight in the refrigerator — do not blend.
4. Mix graham cracker crumbs, melted butter, and sugar together until it resembles wet sand. Press firmly into a small lined pan to form a compact block. Bake at 350F for 10–12 minutes (watch carefully — it burns quickly). Cool, then refrigerate overnight.
5. Next day: remove the banana flesh pieces from the base (they hold together well — just lift out). Reserve base liquid only.
6. Fill one Ninja Creami pint with the strained base. Pour the rest into the traditional ice cream machine and churn.
7. Cut the chilled graham cracker slab into ~1cm cubes.
8. Warm the dulce de leche briefly in the microwave to make it pourable.
9. **For the churned version:** Add graham cracker cubes during the last few minutes of churning. Layer ice cream, dulce de leche, and cookie crumb pieces when filling tubs. Freeze to harden.
10. **For the Ninja Creami:** Freeze the filled pint for at least 24 hours. Spin on Ice Cream mode. Add cookie crumb cubes and drizzle dulce de leche on top after spinning — do not run Mix-In (the buttery crumb and dulce de leche will disintegrate completely in the machine).

**Notes:**
- The banana is used purely for flavor infusion — no banana texture in the final ice cream. Banana extract can substitute if you prefer, but roasting gives a more natural, rounded result.
- Isoamyl acetate is the compound responsible for banana flavor; roasting encourages it to release along with surface sugars.
- Use real South American dulce de leche (thick, pale, creamy). Eagle Brand canned dulce de leche is thin, tastes burnt, and is not recommended.
- Golden syrup or golden corn syrup is preferred over plain corn syrup for an additional flavor note.
- The graham cracker crust sugar melts during baking and creates a more cohesive slab that cuts into clean cubes without crumbling too fine.
- The Ninja Creami version melts faster than the churned version due to lack of air overrun — serve quickly once scooped.
- Nick notes the churned version has better finesse and melt behavior; the Creami version is denser and fudgier but still tastes great.
