### [Baileys Ice Cream — Häagen Dazs Baileys Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a5oA0oePmis)
- **500g** cream or Baileys-flavored extra thick cream
- **150g** sugar (if using Baileys cream, which contains ~50g sugar; use 190g sugar if using plain cream)
- **65g** skimmed milk powder
- **40g** Baileys Irish Cream liqueur (or store-own Irish cream liqueur)
- **1g** xanthan gum

**Steps:**

1. Mix the sugar and skimmed milk powder together dry, combining them thoroughly so they are well incorporated.
2. Pour the milk into a saucepan and begin heating over medium heat.
3. Add the sugar-powder mixture into the warming milk — it dissolves almost instantly when pre-mixed this way.
4. Spoon in the thick Baileys-flavored cream and stir continuously until fully emulsified and dissolved.
5. Remove from heat, transfer to a container, and allow to cool slightly before refrigerating for 3–4 hours until completely chilled.
6. Stir in the Baileys Irish Cream liqueur and give the base a final mix.
7. Churn in an ice cream machine — took approximately 12 minutes (alcohol slows freezing; yours may take slightly longer).
8. Transfer churned ice cream to a tub and freeze overnight to harden fully.
9. Scoop and serve.

**Notes:**

- The Baileys-flavored extra thick cream has higher butterfat than standard UK double cream; the fat layer forming on top after chilling confirms this.
- Nick used a store-own Irish cream liqueur rather than branded Baileys — cheaper and, in his opinion, better flavour.
- Alcohol lowers the freezing point: the ice cream won't go as hard as a non-alcoholic batch, making it easier to scoop straight from the freezer.
- Safe alcohol threshold for churnable ice cream is roughly 75ml per litre of mix. Adding more requires reducing sugar to compensate for the extra PAC (anti-freezing power), which affects solids balance.
- If not using the Baileys-flavored cream, increase sugar from 150g to 190g to compensate for the missing sugar content in the cream.
