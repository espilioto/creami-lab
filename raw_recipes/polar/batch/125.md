### [Salted Malted Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ggTCquqtfEM)

Salt & Straw's salted malted chocolate chip cookie dough ice cream — molasses-vanilla base with malted cookie dough pieces and a malted chocolate fudge swirl.

**Ingredients — Ice Cream Base:**
- **620g** whole milk
- **495g** heavy cream (33–36% fat)
- **200g** sugar
- **92g** molasses
- **12g** glucose syrup (or corn syrup or golden syrup)
- **68g** skim milk powder
- **1.5g** guar gum (or xanthan gum)
- **12g** vanilla extract

**Ingredients — Malted Cookie Dough:**
- **60g** all-purpose flour (heat-treated: bake at 175°C / 350°F for 10 min, then cool completely)
- **60g** unsalted butter, room temperature
- **30g** sugar
- **55g** brown sugar
- **5g** salt
- **30g** heavy cream
- **5g** molasses
- **15g** corn syrup (or glucose or golden syrup)
- **5g** vanilla extract
- **30g** malt powder (Horlicks, dry malt extract, or plain Ovaltine — not chocolate)
- **50g** semi-sweet chocolate chips

**Ingredients — Malted Chocolate Fudge:**
- **15g** cold water
- **10g** malt powder (Horlicks; Nick uses ~3x the recipe amount for stronger malt flavor)
- **110g** corn syrup (or golden syrup or glucose)
- **120g** heavy cream
- **15g** unsalted butter
- **1g** salt
- **20g** cocoa powder
- **85g** chopped chocolate (or chocolate chips)

**Steps:**

1. **Ice cream base:** Combine skim milk powder, guar gum, and sugar in a container and stir together. Add whole milk, cream, molasses, glucose syrup, and vanilla extract. Whisk for 1–2 minutes until sugar is dissolved and everything is fully combined. Refrigerate overnight.

2. **Cookie dough — heat-treat flour:** Spread 60g flour on a tray and bake at 175°C / 350°F for 10 minutes to kill pathogens. Cool completely before using.

3. **Cookie dough:** Cream together butter, sugar, brown sugar, and salt until lumps are broken up. Add cream (30g), vanilla (5g), molasses (5g), and corn syrup (15g); stir to combine. Add cooled heat-treated flour and malt powder; stir together. Fold in chocolate chips. Spread onto foil-lined tray and refrigerate overnight. Before use, chop into small cubes.

4. **Malted fudge:** Stir malt powder into cold water to hydrate. Add corn syrup, cream, butter, salt, and cocoa powder to a small saucepan. Heat over medium, stirring, until mixture comes to a gentle boil and butter has melted. Remove from heat, add chopped chocolate, and stir until completely melted and smooth. Whisk to ensure cocoa powder is fully incorporated. Pour into a container and refrigerate overnight (keeps at least 2 weeks refrigerated).

5. **Churn:** Give the chilled base a final stir to homogenize. Churn in a pre-chilled ice cream machine. Near the end of the churn, add cookie dough pieces and let the machine incorporate them. If the machine isn't powerful enough, fold in by hand.

6. **Pack tubs:** Layer ice cream into containers a few spoonfuls at a time, followed by spoonfuls of malted fudge. Add extra cookie dough pieces as desired. Freeze a few hours to harden.

**Notes:**
- Batch: recipe as written yields ~1.5 kg base; Nick got 2L finished ice cream (Musso 4080 churner). Scale down proportionally for home use.
- Ninja Creami adaptation: make the base, spin it up, then stir in cookie dough pieces by hand, followed by the malted fudge swirl. Warm the fudge slightly before swirling — it is thick when cold.
- The fudge from the original Salt & Straw recipe has a slight iciness when frozen; Nick notes he would add more anti-freeze agent if making it his own way.
- Malt powder options: Horlicks (original, not chocolate), dry malt extract, or plain Ovaltine. Adjust quantity to taste — more malt = more depth.
- Adjust fudge consistency: add more cream for thinner, reduce cream for thicker.
- Do not skimp on chocolate chips in the cookie dough — they provide important textural contrast.
- Base is a molasses-vanilla style — the molasses adds depth and color, not a pronounced treacle flavor.
- Original recipe source: https://tasty.co/recipe/salted-malted-chocolate-chip-cookie-dough-ice-cream-by-salt-and-straw
