### [How to make Dondurma Turkish Ice Cream at home FINAL](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ap5Wg4fNfro)

Luxury stretchy Dondurma using glucomannan (Konjac root flour) as a salep substitute — no salep, no mastic needed.

**Ingredients:**
- **450g** milk (2% fat)
- **350g** cream (33–36% fat)
- **120g** sugar
- **40g** dextrose (or 50g additional sugar)
- **40g** SMP (skim milk powder)
- **4g** glucomannan (Konjac root flour / Konjac flour)
- **1** vanilla bean (split and scraped)

**Steps:**
1. Combine sugar, dextrose, and glucomannan in a bowl and stir together (dry mixing first helps prevent clumping).
2. Add milk and cream to a pot. Pour in the dry mix and the skim milk powder. Stir to combine.
3. Add the vanilla bean pod and scraped seeds to the pot.
4. Heat on the hob, stirring continuously, until the mixture reaches 85–90°C (185–195°F). Hold and stir for a few minutes to fully activate the glucomannan starch.
5. Pour into a container, remove vanilla bean pod after infusing, and refrigerate overnight.
6. After chilling, the base will be thick and viscous. Stir well, then churn in an ice cream machine for ~10 minutes.
7. Scoop into a freezer container and freeze overnight.
8. Before serving, remove from freezer and let temper at room temperature for 5–10 minutes (target serving temp ~-9 to -10°C).
9. Wearing food-safe gloves, work the ice cream by hand (beating and stretching) for 1–2 minutes. The heat from your hands plus the warmed temperature allows the glucomannan starches to reform and develop maximum stretch.
10. Serve immediately.

**Notes:**
- Glucomannan is the active polysaccharide in traditional salep (Turkish orchid root flour) — Konjac root flour contains the same compound and is the recommended substitute.
- Higher heating temp (85–90°C) is required compared to typical stabilizers because glucomannan is a starch and needs more heat to fully hydrate and activate.
- The beat-and-stretch step at the end is essential — skipping it results in far less stretch.
- Ice cream should be served at ~-9 to -10°C, significantly warmer than straight-out-of-freezer temperature; this is authentic to street-vendor style Turkish dondurma.
- Mastic is an optional traditional flavoring for stretchiness; Nick finds it unnecessary with glucomannan.
- Vanilla is not a traditional Dondurma flavor (rose water is more authentic), but it works well here.
- This is a churned recipe (ice cream machine required), not a Ninja Creami recipe.
