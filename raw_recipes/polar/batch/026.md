### [Ben & Jerry's Cherry Garcia - Ice Cream Perfection](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=37S4AS70O_Y)
- **300ml** milk (1.8% fat)
- **80g** sugar
- **60g** cherry concentrate (homemade — see Steps)
- **2 tsp** cherry liqueur (optional)
- **2 tbsp** skimmed milk powder
- **50g** 70% dark chocolate, chopped
- **50g** Opie's black cherries in Luxardo kirsch, chopped (Ben & Jerry's use Bing Cherries; UK version uses maraschino/Luxardo)

**Steps:**

1. Make the cherry concentrate: remove stalks from fresh cherries, place in a pan with a little water and a little sugar. Bring to a rolling boil for 5–10 minutes to soften and release the juice.
2. Strain out the cherry flesh. Return the syrup to the pan and reduce at a rolling boil for a further 5–10 minutes. Optional: add a small amount of pectin during this second boil for more body. Decant into a small container and set aside.
3. Combine milk and sugar in a pan. Heat to dissolve the sugar. Stir in the skimmed milk powder and cherry concentrate. Adjust quantity of concentrate until you reach the desired deep pink colour.
4. Remove from heat and stir in the cream — this cools the mix quickly so it can go straight into the fridge without temperature concerns.
5. Chill the base completely in the fridge (2–4 hours).
6. Churn in an ice cream machine for 15–30 minutes depending on your machine.
7. Once churned, fold in the chopped Opie's black cherries and chopped chocolate.
8. Layer into a container, seal, and freeze for several hours (or overnight/multiple days) until fully set.

**Notes:**

- The cherry concentrate is critical — the original Ben & Jerry's base is quite mild in cherry flavour, so a deeply reduced, punchy syrup is needed to get real cherry taste into the base.
- Cherry variety makes a big difference to flavour intensity. UK cherries can be bland; reduce harder and longer if needed. Bing cherries (used by B&J originally) are preferred but hard to find in the UK.
- Opie's black cherries soaked in Luxardo kirsch are used here instead of plain maraschino cherries — they don't freeze as hard as plain cherries soaked in sugar syrup (a lesson from the first failed attempt).
- The US version of Cherry Garcia uses fudge flecks; the UK version uses chocolate chunks — this recipe goes with chopped 70% chocolate.
- Adding the cream cold into the hot base (rather than heating it) lets you skip a long cooling wait before refrigerating.
