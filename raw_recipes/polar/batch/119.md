### [Making Orange Ice Cream from Bad to Good](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rr-5QuzHejc)
A subscriber's failed orange ice cream is diagnosed and rebuilt with a rind-infused base and proper invert sugar technique.

**Subscriber's Original Recipe (the "bad" version):**
- **500g** milk
- **400g** cream
- **160g** sugar (sucrose)
- **65g** SMP (skim milk powder)
- **200g** orange juice (from ~5 medium-large oranges, reduced)
- **20g** sugar (added to reduction)
- **1g** stabilizer (includes emulsifier)
- **1** medium egg (or Silk Gel — mono and diglycerides of fatty acids — as emulsifier substitute)

**Nick's Improved Recipe:**
- **3** large oranges (juiced — yields ~200g strained juice; roll first to loosen flesh)
- **160g** sugar (sucrose), added to juice
- **20g** sugar (sucrose), reserved for second stage
- **65g** SMP (skim milk powder)
- Rind of **1** large orange (for infusion)
- (Cream and milk quantities carried over from original base; balanced using an ice cream calculator)

**Steps:**
1. Juice 3 large oranges (roll on the counter first to maximise yield). Strain through a sieve into a pan — discard pulp bits.
2. Add 160g sugar to the strained juice. Heat and reduce by half (from ~200g down to ~100g by weight). This creates an invert sugar. Watch constantly — it boils over fast. Takes up to an hour at a heavy simmer.
3. Transfer the 100g reduction to a clean pan. Add the reserved 20g sugar. Heat to a suitable temperature.
4. Add SMP and dissolve fully (stir well; different powders take different times).
5. Peel the rind from 1 large orange (whole strips, not grated). Add to the hot base. Leave the heat low for a couple of minutes to infuse the essential oils into the base. The flavor lives in the rind oils, not the juice.
6. Turn off heat. Transfer to a container (rind left in overnight to steep further). Refrigerate overnight.
7. Remove rind before churning. Churn in an ice cream machine, then freeze.

**Notes:**
- The original recipe failed because it used only juice — citrus flavor comes primarily from essential oils in the rind, not the juice. Juice alone produces almost no orange flavor in ice cream.
- The reduction step creates an invert sugar (sucrose hydrolysed by citric acid in the OJ into glucose + fructose). Glucose and fructose have higher FPD (freezing point depression) than sucrose — this softens scoopability. The original recipe froze too hard/soft for the machine to handle due to imbalance.
- The original also had a gummy/slimy texture — caused by recipe imbalance, not excess stabilizer.
- Silk Gel (mono and diglycerides of fatty acids, E471) was used in the original test batch as an egg substitute/emulsifier. Nick notes eggs are easier to source and do the same job (lecithin in yolk emulsifies fats and water).
- If making again, Nick would also grate some zest directly into the base for even stronger flavor.
- Technique applies to any citrus: swap orange for lemon, lime, etc. — same method.
- Pairs well as a palate cleanser alongside rich chocolate desserts.
