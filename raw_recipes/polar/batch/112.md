### [Making Häagen-Dazs Belgian Chocolate in 2025](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wfpRaBd_Qu0)

Egg-free Häagen-Dazs Belgian Chocolate copycat — rich cocoa base with chopped Belgian chocolate stirred in at the end of the churn. **Batch recipe (makes 2L/2kg).**

**Ingredients:**

- **840g** double cream (33–36% fat; heavy cream or whipping cream)
- **640g** whole milk
- **215g** sugar (195g granulated/caster + 20g vanilla sugar; or all regular sugar + 1 tsp vanilla extract)
- **107g** dextrose
- **100g** skimmed milk powder
- **95g** dark cocoa powder (milk-chocolate-profile cocoa strongly recommended — Nick uses a specific UK brand for the authentic Häagen-Dazs flavour; do NOT use a dark/bitter cocoa profile)
- **200g** 70% Belgian chocolate (Callebaut dark 70% recommended; used as mix-in, chopped — ~100g per litre of ice cream)
- **2g** stabilizer (optional)

**Steps:**

1. Combine cream, milk, sugar (including vanilla sugar), skimmed milk powder, and dextrose in a pot. If using a stabilizer, mix it into the sugar or dextrose first before adding.
2. Heat on the hob to 45–50°C, stirring to dissolve the dry ingredients.
3. Add the cocoa powder and whisk in. Some lumps are normal at this stage.
4. Continue heating to 85°C (with stabilizer) or 60–65°C (without stabilizer) until cocoa is fully dispersed and the mix is smooth.
5. Transfer to a container, let rest on the surface for ~10 minutes, then refrigerate overnight.
6. Next day, whisk the chilled base to reincorporate any settled cocoa solids.
7. Pour into a pre-chilled ice cream machine (run machine 10 minutes before churning). Churn according to machine instructions.
8. At the end of the churn, fold in roughly chopped Callebaut 70% Belgian chocolate (about 100g per litre).
9. Transfer to freezer containers and harden for a minimum of 5 hours before serving.

**Notes:**

- The cocoa powder is the most critical ingredient — Nick uses a UK milk-chocolate-profile cocoa (not available in North America; can be sourced from Amazon). A dark cocoa will still taste good but won't replicate the Häagen-Dazs profile exactly.
- Cocoa powder does not truly dissolve — it disperses into particles. Heating to sufficient temperature ensures full dispersion; stirring/whisking the chilled base before churning is important to reincorporate settled solids.
- Dextrose amount is important for texture and scoopability — do not substitute or adjust casually.
- This is a batch recipe (2L/2kg) — scale down proportionally for smaller containers.
- Callebaut 70% dark chocolate is the recommended brand for the mix-in pieces.
