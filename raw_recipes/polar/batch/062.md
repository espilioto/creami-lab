### [Eggnog that can ALSO be Ice Cream — Eggnog Ice Cream Recipe](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5VIvfFZVohI)

A science-based eggnog custard engineered to double as a churnable ice cream — uses 40DE glucose syrup to balance freezing point and solids without over-sweetening.

**Ingredients:**
- **300g** whole milk (3% fat)
- **250g** whipping cream (33% fat)
- **60g** skimmed milk powder
- **80g** sugar
- **60g** glucose syrup (40DE)
- **100g** egg yolks
- Vanilla extract (to taste, added off-heat)
- Nutmeg (to finish/garnish)

**Steps:**
1. Combine sugar, egg yolks, and glucose syrup (40DE) in a saucepan and begin mixing — the glucose is thick/sticky; warm it briefly in the microwave a few seconds to loosen if needed.
2. Add skimmed milk powder and mix in.
3. Add milk and cream, then heat over the hob to 65–70°C, stirring continuously. At this temperature the egg yolk proteins set and thicken the custard properly.
4. Remove from heat. Once the mix cools to around 50°C or below, stir in vanilla extract (adding it while hot burns off the alcohol that carries the flavor).
5. Chill the base thoroughly in the refrigerator.
6. To use as eggnog: serve chilled, dusted with nutmeg. Add booze on the side if desired — do not add alcohol to the base you intend to freeze.
7. To use as ice cream: decant the required amount into your ice cream machine and churn. Transfer to a tub and freeze.

**Notes:**
- The 40DE glucose syrup is the key functional ingredient — it provides solids and freezing point depression (FPD) without adding sweetness proportional to sucrose (40DE is only ~40–45% as sweet as sugar). Nick states there is no real substitute for it in this recipe.
- Calculator metrics for the base: ~14% fat, ~11% sugar, ~11.7% MSNF (milk solids-non-fat), ~43.3% total solids, ~56.7% water — all within standard ice cream ranges except sugar (slightly low, offset by the glucose syrup FPD).
- Standard eggnog fails as ice cream because booze in the base depresses the freezing point too far — either won't freeze or freezes very icy. Keep alcohol out of any portion destined for churning; serve it on the side instead.
- Always add vanilla extract at or below 50°C to preserve flavor; the alcohol in extract carries the vanilla compounds and evaporates quickly on the hob.
- This recipe makes more than one churn batch — portion off what you need for freezing and keep the rest refrigerated as drinkable eggnog.
- Nick references a boozy eggnog ice cream recipe from 2022 as an alternative if you want alcohol incorporated.
