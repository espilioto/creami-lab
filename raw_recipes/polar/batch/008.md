### [Alcohol in ice cream - Peanut Butter Baileys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gqMO7Hb4yeM)

Peanut butter Baileys gelato with no added sugar — uses homemade peanut butter Baileys as the base liquid, churned traditionally. Episode 1 of Nick's Alcoholic Ice Cream series.

**Ingredients:**

- **357g** milk (Nick uses 2%)
- **330g** homemade peanut butter Baileys (Baileys Irish Cream blended with peanut butter — pre-made)
- **58g** skim milk powder
- **1.5g** guar gum
- **3g** stevia drops

**Steps:**

1. Prepare your homemade peanut butter Baileys in advance by blending Baileys Irish Cream with peanut butter.
2. Combine the skim milk powder and guar gum in a small pot and whisk them together dry.
3. Add the milk and the peanut butter Baileys to the pot. Heat on the hob over high heat, stirring continuously, until the fats melt and the mixture becomes homogeneous (target ~50°C). Do not bring to a full boil.
4. Remove from heat. Add the stevia drops and stir through.
5. Chill in the fridge for 1–2 hours until cold.
6. Churn in an ice cream machine until thick and aerated.
7. Transfer to a container and harden in the freezer.

**Notes:**

- Baileys is 17% ABV — a lower-alcohol liqueur than spirits (40% ABV). Lower ABV means less freezing-point depression, making it more tractable in ice cream than straight spirits.
- Alcohol affects freezing point significantly — you can't simply guess the impact. Different alcoholic drinks vary widely; always calculate rather than estimate.
- If you substitute plain Baileys for the homemade peanut butter Baileys, you will miss the fat contribution from the peanut butter. In that case, add some cream to compensate.
- Guar gum is the only stabilizer used here. Nick notes it has both stabilizing and mild emulsifying properties, making it well-suited for low-temperature bases without added sugar.
- Stevia drops (250–300x sweeter than sugar) are added off the heat. Weigh carefully on a precision scale — 3g is the target. Nick warns that over-use gives a funky off-taste.
- This recipe is intentionally low in total solids (~31%) — the Baileys contributes alcohol but not much solid mass. Nick acknowledges this and uses guar gum to compensate for mouthfeel.
- Result: smooth, creamy peanut butter up front, with Irish whiskey warmth on the finish after swallowing.
- This is a churned gelato recipe, not a Ninja Creami recipe.
