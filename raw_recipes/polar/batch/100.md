### [Making Amazing Alcohol Free Blueberry Extract](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bk_AFHlxlxI)

Technique video: how to make a glycerin-based (alcohol-free) blueberry extract using freeze-dried blueberries and vegetable glycerin thinned with water.

**Ingredients:**

- **250ml** vegetable glycerin (food grade; brands shown: Lauran from Michaels craft store, NOW Solutions from Amazon)
- **60ml** water
- **120g** freeze-dried blueberries (from Amazon; can substitute fresh with a dehydrator — reduce water if using fresh)

**Steps:**

1. Combine vegetable glycerin and water in a small saucepan. Stir well — the mixture will go slightly slimy at first; this is normal. Water is needed to thin the glycerin so it can permeate into the fruit flesh.
2. Add the freeze-dried blueberries to the pan (whole pack for maximum strength).
3. Heat on the stovetop until nearly boiling (80–90°C / 180–190°F), then reduce to the lowest heat setting.
4. Simmer on lowest heat for at least 1 hour. The liquid will turn very dark, almost black.
5. Transfer to an airtight container (Tupperware or bottle).
6. Store at room temperature for approximately 1 month to continue extracting. Alternatively, refrigerate — Nick refrigerated his batch for just over a month.
7. Strain through cheesecloth before use. Yields approximately 250ml of extract.

**Notes:**

- This technique works for any freeze-dried fruit — same ratios, same process, just strain at the end.
- Vegetable glycerin is too thick on its own to extract flavor; the water addition reduces viscosity and enables permeation. Use 100% food-grade VG; organic is preferable.
- Glycerin provides the same freezing point suppression benefit as alcohol, making this extract directly suitable for ice cream and gelato bases.
- If using fresh fruit instead of freeze-dried, reduce the added water since fresh fruit already contains water.
- Use glycerin extract in place of alcohol-based extracts 1:1 in ice cream recipes, coffees, and baking.
