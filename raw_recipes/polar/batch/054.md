### [Malted Vanilla Ice Cream (Whoppers/Maltesers)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A4LRnEkuFXs)

Classic malt shop-style vanilla ice cream with dry malt extract and a stracciatella-style chocolate swirl, topped with Whoppers or Maltesers.

**Ingredients:**
- **250g** whole milk
- **500g** heavy cream
- **3** large egg yolks (OR 1.2g stabilizer)
- **150g** sugar
- **50g** dry malt extract (or liquid malt extract; malted milk powder works 1:1 but weaker flavor)
- **5g** vanilla extract (can use up to 10g)
- Pinch of salt
- **~7g** (1.5 tsp) deodorized/refined coconut oil (for stracciatella)
- Dark chocolate (55%, e.g. Belcolade) — amount not specified; enough to drizzle as stracciatella

**Steps:**
1. Combine milk, cream, sugar, malt extract, salt, and stabilizer (or egg yolks) in a saucepan.
2. Heat over medium, stirring, to 75°C if using egg yolks, or 85°C if using stabilizer.
3. Remove from heat. Cool 5–10 minutes, then stir in vanilla extract.
4. Refrigerate at least 4 hours until fully chilled.
5. Churn in a pre-chilled ice cream machine.
6. Melt chocolate with refined coconut oil until fully liquid.
7. Near the end of the churn, drizzle in the chocolate mixture to create stracciatella shards.
8. Dish into containers and freeze overnight.
9. Serve with a Whopper or Malteser on top.

**Notes:**
- Dry malt extract can clump if stored; lumps dissolve when heated — not a problem.
- Malted milk powder (popular in the US) can substitute 1:1 for dry malt extract but delivers a milder malt flavor.
- If in Europe, liquid malt extract from supermarkets is a fine substitute for dry malt extract.
- Do not boil the dairy base — dairy denatures at 80–85°C; boiling bitters the flavor.
- Whoppers have a stronger malt flavor and lighter texture; Maltesers are denser with European-style chocolate.
- Whoppers/Maltesers cannot be mixed into the base — the malt center dissolves in moisture. Use them as garnish only.
- The chocolate stracciatella is added right at the end of churning (stretchyella technique).
- Recipe yields good overrun; scoops straight from the freezer without tempering.
