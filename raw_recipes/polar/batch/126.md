### [Making Salt & Straw's Honey Lavender Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2sjo6_BRGU4)

A faithful recreation of Salt & Straw's signature honey lavender ice cream using their standard base, stabilized with xanthan gum only.

**Ingredients:**

*Lavender honey syrup:*
- **86g** wildflower honey
- **20g** dried food-grade lavender buds
- **180g** water
- **10 drops** natural purple food coloring (optional)

*Ice cream base:*
- **100g** granulated sugar
- **30g** skimmed milk powder
- **1.4g** xanthan gum
- **30g** corn syrup (not high-fructose)
- **315g** whole milk (~2.4% fat)
- **315g** heavy cream (~37% fat)

**Steps:**

1. Combine honey and water in a saucepan. Bring to a full boil, stirring occasionally.
2. Turn off heat. Add dried lavender buds and steep for a minimum of 4 hours, ideally overnight.
3. Strain the lavender syrup through cheesecloth. Refrigerate until needed.
4. For the ice cream base: combine corn syrup and milk in a pan. Warm gently — no boiling, no steaming.
5. Pre-mix xanthan gum into the sugar (prevents clumping). Add the sugar/xanthan blend and milk powder to the warm milk. Stir until fully combined.
6. Remove from heat. Add cream. Refrigerate until cold.
7. Combine chilled lavender syrup with the ice cream base. Add purple food coloring if using. Stir to combine.
8. Churn in an ice cream machine until scoopable consistency (~11-12 minutes, machine-dependent). Do not stop early — churn until you can scoop it from the machine.
9. Transfer to tub and freeze.

**Notes:**

- This is the Salt & Straw standard base — xanthan gum is the only stabilizer used, which is intentional and true to the original recipe.
- Pre-mixing xanthan gum into the dry sugar before adding to liquid is key to avoiding lumps.
- The lavender syrup can be double-strained for a cleaner result, but a single strain through cheesecloth is sufficient.
- Purple food coloring is optional but strongly recommended — the visual cue sets flavor expectation and enhances the eating experience.
- Dried food-grade lavender buds can be expensive depending on location; only make this if you enjoy floral flavors.
- Tyler Malek (Salt & Straw) stops the churn slightly early in his videos — Nick recommends churning longer until the ice cream is scoopable directly from the machine.
