### [Mascarpone Cherry Gelato](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UgE2Hgwgx1Q)
Homemade recreation of a Waitrose mascarpone amarena cherry gelato — lower sugar, with a Morello cherry swirl instead of the overly sweet store-bought amarena sauce.

**Batch:** Yes — yields multiple tubs (~2kg base)

**Ingredients:**

Gelato base:
- **1227g** milk (1.8% fat)
- **200g** mascarpone cream cheese
- **358g** sugar
- **110g** liquid glucose
- **80g** skimmed milk powder
- **5g** stabilizer

Cherry swirl:
- **400g** Morello cherry compote/sauce (jarred, off the shelf)
- **30g** liquid glucose

**Steps:**

1. Make the cherry swirl first: empty the jar of Morello cherry compote into a pan, add 30g liquid glucose, and warm over heat. Stir until combined and emulsified. Remove from heat and strain through a sieve — liquid only, discard the cherry flesh. Set aside to cool.
2. For the gelato base: add milk to a pan. Add sugar, skimmed milk powder, and stabilizer.
3. Heat the milk mixture. Once it reaches 60–70°C (the activation temperature for the stabilizer), add the mascarpone cheese and stir to incorporate.
4. Once fully combined and smooth, remove from heat. Transfer to a container and refrigerate overnight (or at minimum 4–5 hours) to chill completely.
5. Remove chilled base from fridge and stir well to loosen — mascarpone can settle; it will re-emulsify with mixing.
6. Churn in an ice cream machine until frozen (approx. 18 minutes for this batch size).
7. Load tubs by layering: spoon of churned gelato base, spoon of cherry sauce, repeat until tub is full.
8. Freeze until hardened off.

**Notes:**

- High sugar content (the store original was 20% sugar) means the mix takes longer to churn and will be quite soft straight from the freezer — this is by design for gelato.
- Morello cherries are more tart than amarena cherries; they pair better with the rich mascarpone base and balance the sweetness.
- Cherry sauce choice is flexible: use sweet amarena sauce for a sweeter ripple, Morello for a more balanced/tart result.
- A stabilizer is strongly recommended for large batches — it dramatically improves shelf life and texture stability over time.
- Use a large enough pan; the full batch volume is tight.
- The video was filmed well in advance of publishing; Nick confirmed the finished tubs held up very well in the freezer long-term due to the stabilizer.
