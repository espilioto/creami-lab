### [Making Amazing Chocolate Waffle Cones at Home](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xOh--hwq9DY)

Vegan chocolate waffle cone batter mixed with olive oil and refined coconut oil, pressed at 180°C for ~1 min 25 sec.

**Ingredients** (makes ~4–5 cones, batch size depends on 60–65g batter per cone):
- **80g** all-purpose flour
- **20g** cocoa powder (Ghirardelli recommended — use a good quality one)
- **50g** sugar
- **140g** water
- **20g** olive oil
- **20g** refined coconut oil (refined, not virgin — avoids coconut flavour)

**Steps:**
1. Whisk together flour, cocoa powder, and sugar to break up any lumps.
2. Combine water and olive oil. Melt coconut oil in the microwave for a few seconds, then stir into the water mixture.
3. Add wet ingredients to dry and whisk until smooth — consistency should resemble a thick ice cream base.
4. Refrigerate batter for at least 1 hour (up to 2 days) to fully hydrate the flour and thicken to a semi-viscous, scoopable consistency. Batter must not be runny.
5. Heat waffle iron to 180°C (350°F). Lightly oil plates with olive oil or spray oil.
6. Scoop 60–65g (approx. 1/4 cup) of batter onto an 8-inch waffle iron plate.
7. Cook for 1 minute 25 seconds — can extend to 1 minute 50 seconds, but do not exceed or it burns.
8. Remove immediately with a spatula and quickly roll into a cone shape using a roller/former. Hold until cool enough to retain shape.
9. Stand upright in a glass or cone stand; optionally refrigerate briefly (no more than 30 seconds) to set shape.
10. Optional: seal the tip with a mini marshmallow or a drop of melted chocolate once the cone is fully cooled.

**Notes:**
- Equipment: stovetop waffle cone iron (~$22 CAD on Amazon) works just as well as a dedicated waffle press (Vevor press linked in description). Both use the same batter and temperature.
- Cone former: the plastic former included with most irons works fine. Stainless steel versions exist but are expensive (~$150). A rolled paper cone or 3D-printed form also works.
- Batter thickness is critical — too thin and the cone is fragile and crumbles; thick batter (soft-serve consistency) produces a sturdy, crispy cone.
- To close the tip without a filler: fold the edge just short of the disc's perimeter so the last bit of the disc rolls in on itself when shaping.
- Refrigerating the batter 2 days in advance is fine and may improve texture.
- Refined coconut oil is specified to avoid coconut flavour; olive oil adds neutral richness.
