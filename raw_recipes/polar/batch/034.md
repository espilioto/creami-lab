### [Butterscotch Honeycomb Ice Cream DIY: Step-by-Step Guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cCIrNFq4QHQ)
- **350g** cream (33% fat)
- **80g** brown sugar
- **10g** dextrose
- **90g** butterscotch sauce (from above)
- **55g** skim milk powder (SMP)
- **1.5g** stabilizer (or 2 egg yolks if no stabilizer)

*Honeycomb Candy (optional mix-in):*
- **200g** white sugar
- **100g** golden syrup (e.g. Rogers, or corn syrup)
- **10g** bicarbonate of soda (baking soda — NOT baking powder)

*Butterscotch Chocolate Crackle (topping):*
- **~50g** 70% dark chocolate + butterscotch chocolate chips (~20–25g chips)
- **~10g** refined coconut oil

---

**Steps:**

1. **Make the butterscotch sauce:** Combine brown sugar, butter, and cream in a saucepan. Bring to a boil, reduce heat, and simmer for 3–4 minutes until flavors meld. Remove from heat and set aside. Do not worry about reaching a specific candy temperature — you are just building flavor, not making candy. Reserve 90g for the base; keep the rest for swirling and topping.

2. **Prepare the dry mix:** Combine the brown sugar, dextrose, and stabilizer (or egg yolks) in a bowl and stir together thoroughly. This prevents the stabilizer from clumping when it hits the liquid.

3. **Make the ice cream base:** Gently warm the milk and cream in a saucepan. Add the milk powder and stir to dissolve. Add the dry sugar/stabilizer mix and stir until fully dissolved. Add 90g of the butterscotch sauce and stir to incorporate. Heat to 80–85°C to ensure the stabilizer hydrates fully. Remove from heat and chill in the fridge.

4. **Make the honeycomb candy:** Add white sugar and golden syrup to a clean dry pan. Warm to 140–150°C (hard crack stage). Remove from heat, add bicarbonate of soda, and stir immediately — the mixture will foam up rapidly. Pour into a lined tray and leave to set completely. Once cooled, break into pieces; crush/powder some for dusting and reserve larger pieces for mix-in and garnish. Store unused honeycomb in an airtight container.

5. **Churn the ice cream:** Churn the chilled base in an ice cream machine. In the last few minutes of churning, briefly microwave the reserved butterscotch sauce (10-second bursts) to loosen it to ~25–30°C pouring consistency.

6. **Pack the container:** Layer the churned ice cream into a tub, drizzling butterscotch sauce between layers. Freeze until completely solid (high sugar content means this takes longer — Nick needed ~4 hours at a slightly colder freezer setting).

7. **Make the crackle topping:** Melt the chocolate and butterscotch chips with the refined coconut oil in the microwave in 10–15 second bursts until smooth (about 1 minute total). Use refined (not virgin) coconut oil to avoid coconut flavor.

8. **Serve:** Scoop into a bowl. Pour the warm butterscotch chocolate crackle over the top — it sets on contact with the cold ice cream. Dust with powdered honeycomb and top with a piece of honeycomb candy.

---

**Notes:**
- Nick notes the butterscotch sauce solidifies overnight at room temperature — it will need a brief microwave to loosen before use.
- The description includes an editorial note: on day 3 in the freezer the high butterfat becomes very noticeable. Nick planned to revise the recipe but states it is beautiful for the first 2 days.
- Critical: use bicarbonate of soda (baking soda), NOT baking powder for the honeycomb. Baking powder produces a flat, dense result with very little aeration.
- The stabilizer can be replaced with 2 egg yolks — egg yolks provide emulsification which is especially important given the high butterfat from the butterscotch sauce.
- Mix the stabilizer with the dry sugars before adding to liquid to prevent clumping.
- This is a traditional churned ice cream recipe, not a Ninja Creami recipe.
