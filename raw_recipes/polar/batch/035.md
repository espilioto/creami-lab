### [Candy Cane Ice Cream: A Festive Frozen Delight](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8MLXd-NAU8I)
Peppermint ice cream base with candy cane pieces churned in — a classic Canadian/North American festive flavour.

**Ingredients:**
- **500g** milk (2% fat)
- **300g** cream (33% fat)
- **90g** sugar
- **50g** dextrose
- **46g** skim milk powder
- **2g** stabilizer (Special Ingredients brand)
- **10g** peppermint extract
- **2–3** candy canes (broken up, dissolved into the hot base)
- **6** candy canes, crushed (frozen, added as mix-in near end of churn)

**Steps:**
1. Crush 6 candy canes in a pestle and mortar to a mix of medium-to-small pieces and powder — not super fine. Put in a container and freeze.
2. Combine milk and cream in a saucepan over low heat. Add sugar, dextrose, skim milk powder, and stabilizer, stirring to dissolve.
3. Break 2–3 candy canes into the warming liquid to dissolve and infuse flavor.
4. Heat to 75–80°C (required to activate the stabilizer). Turn off heat and allow to tick for a few more minutes.
5. Pour base into a container. Cover and refrigerate until fully chilled.
6. Once cold, add peppermint extract if desired (add when cold, not hot — volatile flavour). Note: the dissolved candy canes may provide enough mint flavour on their own; taste before adding extract.
7. Churn in ice cream machine. About 2–3 minutes before end of churn, add the frozen crushed candy cane pieces.
8. Transfer to storage container and freeze.

**Notes:**
- The 2 candy canes dissolved into the base provided sufficient mint flavour without any peppermint extract — taste your base before adding extract.
- Adding more candy canes to the base is possible but they are pure sugar and will affect the freezing point.
- Candy canes sourced from Canadian Tire.
- Nick describes this as a bigger batch he wants to make again — implying it may be a larger-than-single-pint yield (500g milk + 300g cream = ~800g liquid before other solids).
- Recipe was previously used as the base in Nick's "Minter Wonderland" comparison video (2022).
