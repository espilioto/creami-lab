### [Vanilla Ice Cream - Boring or Perfect?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bmqquJnhuls)

Classic custard-based vanilla ice cream made with a churn method, using a dual-sugar base and real vanilla pods.

**Ingredients:**

- **500g** double/whipping cream
- **400g** milk
- **120g** sugar
- **40g** dextrose
- **20g** light brown sugar
- **50g** skimmed milk powder
- **3** medium egg yolks
- **1.5g** stabilizer (pre-mixed into dextrose)
- **1** large plump vanilla pod (or 2 smaller/thinner ones)

**Steps:**

1. Add milk to a pan over gentle heat. Add sugar, dextrose (with stabilizer pre-mixed in), and light brown sugar. Stir to combine.
2. Add egg yolks to the cold/barely warm mixture — this is the safest method to avoid scrambling.
3. Bring temperature up gradually, stirring constantly. Target ~70°C / when the base just starts to steam slightly with no bubbles.
4. Once at temperature, turn off the heat and let the base rest for a few minutes.
5. While resting, split the vanilla pod(s) lengthwise, scrape out the seeds, and add both seeds and pods to the hot custard.
6. Add skimmed milk powder and stir until fully combined.
7. Let the pods infuse for a couple of minutes, then remove the pods. Transfer base to a container and chill in the fridge until completely cold.
8. Once chilled, give the base a final whisk to ensure it is fully emulsified. Pour into an ice cream machine and churn as normal.
9. Once churned, transfer to a tub and freeze for several hours (overnight recommended) before serving.

**Notes:**

- Light brown sugar enhances the caramel notes of vanilla flavor; don't overdo it or the profile shifts toward caramel. It also darkens the color slightly.
- Use 1 large plump pod for best results; 2 thinner pods are an acceptable substitute.
- Adding egg yolks at the start (cold milk) is Nick's preferred method — safer than tempering, avoids scrambling.
- No thermometer? Watch for the first wisps of steam rising from the surface with no bubbles — that's ~70°C.
- The vanilla pods themselves infuse the base even after seeds are removed — leave them in during the chill.
- This base is versatile: add mix-ins or flavors just as commercial producers do.
