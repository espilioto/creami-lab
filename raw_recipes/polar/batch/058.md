### [Devon Cream Tea Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W3dY0p2rQBA)
- **400g** clotted cream (63–67.5% fat — Nick uses Rodda's)
- **170g** sugar
- **50g** dextrose
- **10g** glucose syrup
- **50g** SMP (skim milk powder)
- **1.3g** stabilizer (optional)
- Jam to taste (raspberry, strawberry, or black cherry — Nick uses raspberry)
- Scones, broken into pieces (store-bought or homemade)

**Steps:**

1. Add milk, sugar, dextrose, glucose syrup, and SMP to a pan. Whisk until everything is really well combined before moving on.
2. Add the clotted cream and heat on the hob, stirring, until the cream is fully melted and homogenized into the base.
3. Transfer to a container and chill in the fridge. Note: the high butterfat will solidify on top as it cools — this is normal.
4. Once fully chilled, blend for 10–15 seconds to re-emulsify the fat back into the base before churning.
5. Churn in your ice cream machine. The base will solidify quickly due to the fat content — keep a close eye on it.
6. To tub up: spread scone pieces with jam. Layer churned ice cream, jam-covered scone chunks, and extra jam swirls into your container.
7. Freeze for several hours until fully set.

**Notes:**

- Devon cream tea tradition: cream goes on the scone first, jam on top. Cornish style is jam first.
- Clotted cream cannot truly be replicated at home — the commercial crust is unique. Nick links a homemade recipe in the description for those outside the UK.
- The recipe yields a very fatty, rich base — this is by design, not a mistake.
- Use whatever jam you prefer; strawberry is traditional but raspberry or black cherry work well.
- Nick uses Rodda's clotted cream (67.5% fat).
- The stabilizer is optional — Nick lists 1.3g but notes you don't have to use it.
