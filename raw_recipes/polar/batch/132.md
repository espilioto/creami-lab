### [Japanese Apple Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VrBgrat2kxA)

Poll-winner recreation of an apple-miso ice cream from a street cart in Shibuya, Tokyo — warm umami notes with subtle apple flavour and visible apple flesh flecks.

**Ingredients:**

Apple compote:
- **250g** apple (ideally Fuji; Pink Lady works well)
- **100g** sugar
- **50g** water
- **15g** white miso paste (recipe states ~13g used on camera)

Ice cream base:
- **500g** milk
- **300g** cream
- **70g** sugar (additional)
- **20g** glucose liquid
- **65g** skimmed milk powder

**Steps:**

1. Peel and chop apple into small cubes. Combine with 100g sugar and 50g water in a pan.
2. Cook on medium heat for 30–45 minutes, stirring, until apple breaks down soft and tender.
3. Stir in white miso paste until fully incorporated. Remove from heat and cool completely (a few hours).
4. Transfer cooled apple-miso mixture to a blender. Blitz thoroughly until smooth but with some apple fibre remaining — do not over-process to total smoothness.
5. Combine milk, cream, apple puree, remaining 70g sugar, glucose, and skimmed milk powder. Mix until fully combined and emulsified.
6. If the base splits at this stage: mix a small teaspoon of sugar with a quarter teaspoon of sunflower lecithin, add back into the split base off the heat, and gently whisk until it comes back together. (This rescue works for eggless bases only.)
7. Refrigerate base overnight — apple fibres will swell, giving an unusual thick consistency; this is expected.
8. Give a final stir, then churn in ice cream machine.
9. Transfer to containers and freeze until fully hardened.

**Notes:**

- Fuji apples are preferred (sweeter, authentic to the Tokyo original); Pink Lady or similar sweet apple is a good substitute.
- The base will look unusual and lumpy both before churning and during the churn — this is normal; the end result scoops cleanly with visible apple flecks.
- White miso acts as a flavour enhancer with warm, umami/sesame-like notes rather than a savoury flavour.
- Base splitting is caused by the high fruit-acid content mixing with dairy; the lecithin rescue method works reliably for eggless bases.
- Traditional churned recipe — not a Ninja Creami no-churn recipe.
