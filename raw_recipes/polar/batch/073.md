### [How to Master Häagen-Dazs Belgian Chocolate Ice Cream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WMqQvMxHbzE)

A churned Belgian chocolate ice cream dupe of the Häagen-Dazs classic — rich, simple, and eggless.

**Ingredients:**

- **250ml** double cream (heavy cream)
- **125ml** milk
- **125g** sugar (granulated or caster)
- **100g** Belgian chocolate, 70% (chopped or grated fine)
- **30g** dark cocoa powder (organic recommended)
- **30g** skimmed milk powder

**Steps:**

1. Combine cream, milk, sugar, cocoa powder, and skimmed milk powder in a pot on the hob.
2. Heat, stirring, until sugar is fully dissolved.
3. Add the chopped/grated Belgian chocolate while still on the heat; whisk until fully melted and amalgamated.
4. Remove from heat and refrigerate for 24 hours.
5. After chilling, stir the mix (milk solids may have risen to the top — stir back in).
6. Churn in an ice cream machine until done (~30 minutes). Add any reserved chocolate pieces as a mix-in in the final couple of minutes of churning.
7. Transfer to a tub and freeze for ~4 hours before serving.

**Notes:**

- Use skimmed (no-fat) or at most low-fat milk powder — you want to increase solids, not fat content.
- Chocolate: 70% is the sweet spot. Above ~85% can turn bitter.
- An update from Nick recommends grating the chocolate (rather than chopping) with a fine/small grater for better integration into the mix.
- Any dark Belgian chocolate works — supermarket own-brand is fine, no need for premium couverture.
- This is a traditional churned recipe, not a Ninja Creami recipe.
