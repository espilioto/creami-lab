### [The Best Pineapple Ice Cream to make at home](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j8w_7cgVPqI)

Classic churned pineapple ice cream using a cooked pineapple jam technique to eliminate icy fruit chunks.

**Ingredients:**

Pineapple jam:
- **250g** ripe pineapple, cut into ~1cm cubes
- **150g** sugar
- **30–50g** cold water
- **3g** pectin powder
- **5g** sugar (to mix with pectin before adding)

Ice cream base:
- **500g** double cream (heavy cream)
- **400g** milk (1.8–2% fat)
- **70g** sugar
- **80g** skimmed milk powder

**Steps:**

1. Combine pineapple chunks, 150g sugar, and 30–50g cold water in a pan. Heat to a soft boil and cook for 20–25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until most free water has evaporated.
2. Mix 3g pectin powder with 5g sugar to prevent clumping, then stir into the boiling pineapple mixture. Continue cooking a further 10 minutes. The key sign it's ready: bubbles rise up the pan on their own without stirring — this means free water is stabilised by the sugars and pectin.
3. Remove from heat, cool slightly, transfer to a sealed container and leave to cool completely (Nick left it 24 hours).
4. When pineapple jam is fully chilled, add milk to a pan with 70g sugar and heat until sugar dissolves. To avoid curdling the milk from the pineapple's acidity, add the jam in two stages: first drain some of the sugar syrup from the jam into the warm milk and stir through, then add the remaining pineapple pieces and syrup. Stir well and remove from heat.
5. Transfer to a large container, add the cream, and refrigerate overnight until completely chilled. The pineapple will settle to the bottom — whisk gently to redistribute before churning.
6. Churn in an ice cream machine until done (Nick's machine took 10–12 minutes). Scoop into containers and freeze for 4–5 hours to harden.

**Notes:**

- Use fully ripe pineapple — unripe pineapple will produce hard, icy chunks. The thumb-press test: the skin should yield slightly when pressed.
- The boil-up stage (bubbles rising on their own after stirring) is the critical sign that free water is bound — don't skip it or the pineapple pieces will turn icy in the finished ice cream.
- For a more scoopable result straight from the freezer, substitute some of the table sugar (sucrose) for dextrose or other invert sugars to increase freezing point depression.
- Nick recommends adding a stabilizer blend to keep the ice cream ice-free throughout its freezer life.
- This is not a Ninja Creami recipe — it uses a conventional ice cream machine with a churn step.
