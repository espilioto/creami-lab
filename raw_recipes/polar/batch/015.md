### [Strawberry Gelato with Dark Chocolate Stracciatella](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OLfIEGMkXpM)

Strawberry gelato made with store-bought jam for consistent flavour and balanced solids, finished with dark chocolate stracciatella drizzled in during churning.

**Ingredients:**

- **299g** strawberry jam (pure, no sugar substitutes — check label)
- **64.7g** skim milk powder
- **777.9g** whole milk (3.25%)
- **74.9g** sugar
- **278.4g** cream (33–36%)
- **4g** stabilizer (warm-process type)
- Red gel food coloring (optional, fat-soluble)

*Stracciatella:*
- **100g** dark chocolate (60–70%)
- **15–20g** extra virgin olive oil

**Steps:**

1. Combine milk, cream, strawberry jam, and sugar in a saucepan.
2. Pre-mix stabilizer with the sugar before adding to the base to prevent clumping.
3. Heat the base to 80–85°C and hold for 2–5 minutes to fully hydrate the stabilizer.
4. Remove from heat. Add red gel food coloring if desired.
5. Add skim milk powder off-heat and stir until fully dissolved — Nick found this gave better results than adding it before heating.
6. Strain the base through a fine sieve to remove any jam solids. Transfer to a clean container.
7. Chill in the fridge overnight (or cool rapidly in an ice bath, stirring as it cools).
8. Churn in a pre-chilled ice cream machine until near-done.
9. For stracciatella: chop chocolate, combine with olive oil in a microwave-safe container, and melt in 30-second bursts (approx. 1–1.5 min total). Do not burn. Set aside to cool slightly but keep fluid.
10. Towards the end of the churn, drizzle the melted chocolate in a thin stream into the churning gelato — it freezes on impact, shattering into fine flakes.
11. Transfer to a container, smooth the top, and harden in the freezer for 4–6 hours before serving.

**Notes:**

- This is a traditional (non-Ninja Creami) churned gelato recipe — uses a standard ice cream machine.
- Always check the jam's nutrition label for sugar content — it directly affects freezing point. Avoid jams sweetened with sugar substitutes (sorbitol, maltitol, etc.) as they throw off the balance.
- Use fat-soluble gel food coloring; water-based coloring doesn't distribute evenly in a fat-containing base.
- Pour chocolate in a thin, steady stream for delicate flakes. A thick pour creates chunks. Add generously — Nick says more is better.
- Magic shell can substitute for the chocolate/olive oil mixture but the flavour is inferior.
- Let the finished gelato harden 4–6 hours before serving for best texture and scoop.
- Gelato uses more stabilizer than ice cream (4g here) because the lower fat content requires more structural support.
- Nick tested three variations and found that adding skim milk powder after the heating phase produced the best result.
- Calculator used: icecreamcalc.com (Chrome-based web app).
