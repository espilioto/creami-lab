### [Basil Ice Cream Recipe from 'The Perfect Scoop'](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UiLIo41cbLo)

Basil ice cream adapted from David Lebovitz's *The Perfect Scoop* — made with stabilizer instead of egg yolks, using a traditional ice cream machine.

**Ingredients:**
- **25g** fresh basil
- **150g** sugar
- **250g** whipping cream (blended with basil)
- **250g** heavy cream (combined separately)
- **250g** 2% milk
- **1 pinch** salt
- **ice cream stabilizer** (amount not stated — pre-mixed with a little sugar before adding)
- **zest of 1 lemon**

**Steps:**
1. Combine the basil, sugar, and 250g whipping cream. Blend to as smooth a puree as possible.
2. Divide the blended basil mixture in half. Pour one half into a bowl.
3. Add the 250g heavy cream to the bowl and stir to combine. Set aside in the fridge.
4. Pour the remaining blended half into a pan.
5. Pre-mix the stabilizer with a small amount of sugar (a teaspoon or two) to prevent clumping.
6. Add the 250g milk, a pinch of salt, and the pre-mixed stabilizer to the pan. Stir to combine.
7. Heat the pan on the hob, whisking, until the mixture reaches 80°C (to hydrate the stabilizer).
8. Remove from heat. Add the lemon zest and stir to combine.
9. Combine the heated pan mixture with the cold cream bowl mixture. Stir well.
10. Chill completely in the fridge.
11. Once fully chilled, stir again to recombine, then churn in an ice cream machine (~12 minutes).
12. Transfer to a container and freeze.

**Notes:**
- Nick substituted stabilizer for egg yolks — stabilizer gives a cleaner basil flavor since egg yolks add their own taste that competes with delicate herbs.
- The lemon zest is important: it cuts through and brightens the basil without making it taste lemon-flavored — just a subtle lift at the finish.
- Basil flavor is naturally strong; the dairy fat tones it down to a pleasant, lingering level rather than overpowering.
- Nick made two versions (with and without egg yolks) and preferred the stabilizer version for complexity.
- The heated basil intensifies its green color.
- Pre-mixing any powdered stabilizer with sugar (or milk powder) before adding to liquid is essential — adding it straight to liquid causes clumping and it won't function.
- Classic 2:1 cream-to-milk ratio from the original book recipe.
- Recipe source: David Lebovitz, *The Perfect Scoop*.
