# Polar Ice Creamery — Recipe Archetypes & Baselines

> Distilled from ~44 representative recipes across Nick's 171 Polar Ice Creamery videos.
> Purpose: guide recipe creation, adaptation, and optimization by providing category-specific
> ratios, techniques, and translation rules to our sugar-free/low-fat style.
> Raw transcripts: `raw_recipes/polar/` and `raw_recipes/polar_theory/`

---

## How to Use This File

When creating or adapting a recipe, find its flavor category below. Each archetype gives you:
1. **Traditional baseline** — what a good version looks like with normal ingredients
2. **Key ratios** — how much of the flavor ingredient per pint, relative to base
3. **Technique** — the process that makes it work (ingredient-agnostic)
4. **Translation** — how to hit the same quality targets with our pantry

## Chocolate

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 15-20% | Cocoa fat + cream fat. Chocolate bases run high |
| Sugar% | 14-18% | Needs enough FPD to counter cocoa hardening |
| Solids% | 42-48% | Cocoa powder adds significant solids |
| Stabilizer | 2-3.5g per ~1kg batch | Hot-process (85C) typical |
| SMP | 40-65g per ~1kg batch | Essential for MSNF |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Cocoa powder:** 25-50g (lighter chocolate → 25g, intense dark → 50g)
- **Physical chocolate:** 40-75g if used alongside cocoa. Adds solids fast — don't overdo
- **Dextrose:** 40-60g alongside sugar. Chocolate freezes harder than any other flavor — dextrose is almost mandatory for scoopability
- **Instant coffee/espresso powder:** 0.5-1 tsp. Enhances chocolate depth without tasting like coffee

### Technique
- **Bloom cocoa powder** in hot liquid (50-55C+) — disperses better, brings out full flavor. Cold cocoa = dull, gritty
- **Black cocoa** (12-13% fat) tastes disgusting unless heavily cooked — avoid in cold-process Creami recipes
- Combine cocoa powder + physical chocolate for layered flavor (cocoa = intensity, chocolate = richness)
- High-quality cocoa is non-negotiable: Valrhona, Ghirardelli Dutch, Callebaut, Cacao Barry. Cheap cocoa ruins chocolate ice cream

### Translation to Our Style
- **Fat gap:** Replace cream with 1.5% milk. Lost body from: whey protein (20-30g), inulin (15-25g), MCC (5-8g)
- **Sugar gap:** Erythritol 30-40g + xylitol 15-20g + glycerin 10-15g. The erythritol+xylitol combo replaces both sweetness and FPD. Glycerin adds extra FPD without sweetness
- **Cocoa hardening:** Traditional recipes counter with dextrose (PAC=190). We counter with xylitol (PAC=2.2) and glycerin (PAC=3.7). Must overshoot FPD vs vanilla recipes
- **Flavor:** Cocoa ratios stay the same (25-50g). Bloom in small amount of hot water before mixing into cold base. Add chocolate flavor drops (max 2) for depth
- **Scoopulator:** Expect red on Fat%, Sugars%, MSNF%. Focus on PAC > 22 (higher than vanilla due to cocoa hardening)

### Proven Combos
- Chocolate base + coffee fudge swirl (Nick's Coffee Coffee Buzz Buzz Buzz)
- Cocoa base + dark chocolate stracciatella (100g chocolate + 10g olive oil, drizzled into spinning ice cream)
- Triple chocolate: cocoa in base + melted dark chocolate + chocolate fudge mix-in
- Chocolate + peanut butter: 30-40g PB folded in after spinning
- Chocolate + caramel: dulce de leche swirl (warm to pourable, layer when packing)

## Fruit

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Ice Cream | Sorbet |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Fat% | 10-15% (cream-based) | 0% |
| Sugar% | 14-18% | 22-28% (must be high for texture) |
| Solids% | 35-40% | 25-35% |
| Fruit amount | 100-300g per ~1kg batch | 300-500g per ~1kg batch |
| Stabilizer | 1.5-4g | 2-5g (pectin or guar) |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Fruit:** 100-200g for ice cream, 200-350g for sorbet
- **Jam/preserve** as flavor source: 150-200g. Solves the fresh fruit problem (too much water, inconsistent sugar)
- **Inulin (in sorbet):** 25-35g for solids that sugar-free sweeteners don't provide
- **Corn syrup/invert sugar:** 25-50g. Changes serving temp from -10C to -16C in sorbets — scoopable from freezer

### Technique
- **Fresh fruit problem:** Fresh fruit = lots of water = large ice crystals. Solutions:
  - Cook down fresh fruit on hob to evaporate water and concentrate flavor (strawberries, blueberries, pineapple)
  - Use jam/preserves instead (pre-concentrated, consistent sugar, no excess water)
  - Use frozen fruit (already lost some cell structure water via freezing)
- **Frozen blueberries** work as well as fresh and are much cheaper — simmer and crush to reduce
- **Pineapple:** Make into jam first (250g fruit + 150g sugar + 3g pectin). Watch for the boiling stage — that's when water is stabilized by sugars. If you skip this, you get icy pineapple lumps
- **Mango sorbet:** Each fruit has different sugar/water content — no "one recipe fits all." Mango needs its own balance
- **Hibiscus sorbet:** Steep dried flowers 1 hour, strain out, then add sweetener + stabilizer
- **Balsamic glaze** on strawberry sorbet — classic restaurant pairing

### Translation to Our Style
- **Ice cream:** Replace cream with 1.5% milk + whey (15-20g) + inulin (15-20g). Fruit ratios stay the same
- **Sorbet:** Sugar-free sorbet MUST have 25-35% sweetener content or it becomes "super dry and powdery." Use xylitol (68-80g per pint) + inulin (25-35g for solids) + guar gum (1-2g). Nick's hibiscus sorbet used 240g xylitol + 144g inulin for 1.5L batch
- **Jam shortcut still works:** Use sugar-free jam/preserves if available. Check sugar content carefully
- **Corn syrup substitute:** Glycerin (10-15g) provides similar FPD and texture effect without sugar
- **Scoopulator:** Sorbets will show red on most metrics. Focus on PAC 30-36 range

## Nut / Praline

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 14-22% | Nut fats + cream. Naturally high-fat category |
| Sugar% | 14-18% | Standard range |
| Solids% | 38-48% | Nuts add significant solids |
| Nut paste/butter | 75-156g per ~1kg batch | The defining ingredient |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Pistachio paste/nuts:** 50-75g shelled, processed into paste
- **Hazelnut:** Use hazelnut extract (5g) for ultra-smooth base, praline for mix-in
- **Peanut butter:** 30-50g folded in or as swirl
- **Pecan:** 100-150g toasted in brown butter (50g butter)
- **SMP:** 20-65g (varies widely — higher when nut amount is lower)

### Technique
- **Pistachio paste method:** Blitz shelled nuts with sugar + SMP in blender, then add small amounts of warmed milk (~100ml microwaved) to turn into liquid paste. Do NOT use food processor (overheats, won't get smooth)
- **Hazelnut praline:** Caramelize 100g sugar, add 100g hazelnuts, cool and break up. Serve alongside or mix in after spinning
- **Brown butter (beurre noisette):** 50g butter, medium heat 5-8 min until foam subsides and dark brown solids appear. The "unknown flavor" in great butter pecan. Creates toasted hazelnut-like Maillard flavors
- **Toast nuts before adding** — deepens flavor. Toast dry first, then soak in brown butter
- **Coconut:** Coconut milk (17% fat) + coconut cream (21% fat) as base. Relatively easy to make but hard to make WELL

### Translation to Our Style
- **Fat gap less critical here** — nut fats provide body naturally. Can use less whey/inulin than other categories
- **Pistachio:** Use the paste method with our milk. Reduce paste amount slightly (40-55g) since we're not compensating with cream fat
- **PB:** Our pantry PB (48% fat) provides significant fat and body on its own. 25-35g per pint
- **Sweetener:** Erythritol + xylitol as usual. Nut flavors are strong enough to not get dulled by sweetener cooling effect
- **Brown butter:** Ingredient-agnostic technique — works exactly the same in our recipes. Use for butter pecan or any toasted-nut recipe

## Coffee / Espresso

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 10-18% | Standard cream-based range |
| Sugar% | 14-20% | Coffee bitterness needs adequate sweetness |
| Espresso/coffee | 100-120g brewed espresso per ~1kg batch | Or equivalent instant |
| SMP | 45-60g per ~1kg batch | |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Brewed espresso:** 60-80g in base (adds water — account in total liquid)
- **Instant coffee powder:** 5-15g. Better for boosting intensity without adding water
- **Coffee extract (alcohol-based):** 4-5g per pint. Concentrated, minimal water impact
- **Coffee fudge swirl:** 200g condensed milk + 100g dark chocolate + 10g instant coffee

### Technique
- **Don't increase espresso to boost flavor** — it adds water, throws off balance. Use instant coffee powder or extract instead
- **Steep whole beans** in warm base for 30+ minutes, then strain. Loses ~10g in grounds — top back up
- **Instant coffee** doesn't affect freezing point or ice crystal formation — safe to add freely
- **Coffee + chocolate:** Coffee/espresso powder (0.5-1 tsp) enhances chocolate intensity without tasting like coffee
- **Dual-layer (espresso con panna):** Freeze espresso gelato layer partially, then pour stabilized whipped cream on top, freeze, spin with Top/Bottom mode

### Translation to Our Style
- **Espresso/instant amounts stay the same** — they're already sugar-free
- **Base swap:** 1.5% milk + whey + inulin as usual
- **Sweetener:** Coffee bitterness needs enough sweetness to balance. Erythritol 30-35g + xylitol 15-20g + sucralose pinch. Don't undershoot sweetness or the coffee tastes harsh
- **Coffee fudge:** Can't use condensed milk (sugar). Sub: melt 100g dark chocolate (sugar-free if available) + 10g instant coffee + 15g glycerin + 30g cocoa powder. Different texture but similar intensity
- **Flavor drops:** Milk Cream flavor (1-2 drops) can add richness that missing cream would normally provide

## Caramel / Dulce de Leche

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 7-18% | Wide range (cream-based to DDL-heavy) |
| Sugar% | 18-22% | Caramel inherently sweet — runs higher |
| DDL/caramel sauce | 90-225g per ~1kg batch | Primary flavor source |
| SMP | 30-65g | |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Dulce de leche:** 60-150g depending on intensity. Argentinian DDL (Havana, Vacalin) is less sweet than Eagle brand
- **Pre-made caramel sauce:** 100-150g (Costco salted caramel butter sauce works well)
- **Salt:** 1-3g for salted caramel profile
- **Dextrose:** 40-60g — DDL already has inverted sugars that help scoopability

### Technique
- **Eagle brand DDL "tastes burnt"** — use real South American DDL if possible (theargentino.com)
- **Caramelize allulose** by heating — creates caramel flavor without sugar (Nick's sugar-free salted caramel method)
- **Banoffee method:** Roast bananas (175C, 10-15 min, skins go black) → Maillard reaction deepens flavor → steep flesh in hot base overnight → strain before churning. Genius technique for deep banana flavor without banana solids in the final product
- **Graham cracker base for mix-in:** 200g crumbs + 80g butter + 20g sugar, baked, cooled, cubed to ~1cm. Do NOT use Creami Mix-In cycle for buttery crumbs (they disintegrate) — fold in by hand after spinning

### Translation to Our Style
- **Sugar-free caramel:** Caramelize allulose (we don't have it — substitute: heat erythritol + xylitol mix, but crystallization risk). Or use Milk Cream flavor drops (2 drops) + glycerin (15g) + salt (1-2g) for caramel-like profile
- **DDL substitute:** No direct equivalent in our pantry. Closest: cream cheese (30-50g) + Milk Cream flavor drops + glycerin + pinch salt
- **Banoffee technique is ingredient-agnostic** — roasted banana steeping works with any base liquid
- **Salted caramel Creami:** Nick's recipe: 633g light cream + 80g allulose (caramelized) + 32g SMP + 3g salt + 2g stevia. Our version: 600g 1.5% milk + 20g whey + 40g erythritol + 20g xylitol + 15g glycerin + 2g salt + pinch sucralose + Milk Cream drops

## Cookies & Cream / Cookie Dough

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 12-24% | High range due to butter in cookies/dough |
| Sugar% | 14-20% | |
| Cookie amount | 8-12 Oreos (or equivalent) per ~1kg batch | |
| SMP | 50-68g | |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Oreo cookies:** 4-6 cookies. Remove cream, crush cookie part into base powder + keep pieces for mix-in
- **Biscoff spread:** 80-100g blended into base
- **Cookie dough cubes:** 80-120g, chilled, cut to ~1cm
- **Vanilla extract:** 5-10g. Vanilla base is the standard for cookies & cream

### Technique
- **Oreo method:** Separate cream from cookies. Cream goes INTO the base (adds vanilla + body). Cookie part = some blended into base as powder (flavor), rest as chunky mix-in
- **Biscoff:** Resists mixing in cold liquid — keep stirring. Creami blade destroys all crunchy pieces → ultra-smooth but loses textural contrast
- **Cookie dough food safety:** Flour must be heat-treated (175C, 10 min) to kill pathogens since dough is not cooked further
- **No-cook gelato:** Xanthan gum (1.7-2.5g) hydrates cold → mix and churn within 10 minutes
- **Malt extract** (50g) adds a 1950s diner milkshake vibe to vanilla/cookie bases

### Translation to Our Style
- **Base:** Standard milk + whey + inulin swap. Cookies provide their own flavor
- **Oreos:** Available at supermarket — no swap needed. The cookie itself is the star
- **Biscoff:** Available at supermarket — no swap needed
- **Cookie dough:** DIY with erythritol replacing sugar, our PB if peanut butter cookie dough. Heat-treat flour
- **Malt:** We don't have malt extract — skip or add as buy-list item for malt-forward recipes

## Vanilla / Malt

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 12-18% | Vanilla needs fat to carry flavor |
| Sugar% | 14-17% | |
| Vanilla | 5-40g depending on form | Extract, paste, or bean |
| SMP | 30-67g | |
| Egg yolks | 0-6 (Philly vs custard) | |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Vanilla extract:** 5-10g (our standard)
- **Vanilla bean paste:** 15-25g (far superior to extract but we don't have it)
- **Vanilla bean:** Half bean (~2g) = 6g extract equivalent
- **Malt extract (dry, medium):** 25-35g per pint for malt-forward. Can double for stronger flavor
- **Condensed milk shortcut:** 175-200g per pint replaces sugar + some dairy + provides body (but muddies flavor)

### Technique
- **Custard vs Philly:** Custard (with eggs) = richer body, deeper flavor, more work. Philly (no eggs) = lighter, cleaner vanilla, simpler. Nick prefers custard for vanilla-forward recipes
- **Vanilla bean infusion:** Gently heat milk with sliced bean (add seeds too), steep on lowest heat 1 hour
- **Aging:** 12-24h fridge rest is critical for vanilla — flavor develops significantly overnight
- **Malt:** Use medium or dark malt extract (light doesn't have enough flavor). Horlicks, Ovaltine, or dry malt extract

### Translation to Our Style
- **Vanilla extract** works directly — no swap needed
- **Base:** 1.5% milk + whey (20-25g) + inulin (15-20g) + MCC (5g). Vanilla-forward recipes need more body compensation than other flavors because there's nothing (cocoa, fruit, nuts) masking the thinness
- **Sweetener balance is more critical in vanilla** — any cooling effect from erythritol or off-flavor from sweeteners is fully exposed. Use more xylitol relative to erythritol, and glycerin to smooth the profile. Sucralose pinch for top-end sweetness
- **Condensed milk:** Can't use (sugar). But the principle is sound — you want solids + sweetness + body in one ingredient. Closest in our pantry: cream cheese (30-40g) + sweetener + inulin

## Boozy

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 10-18% | Standard |
| Sugar% | 12-18% | Alcohol already depresses freezing point |
| Alcohol | 50-100g at 17-40% ABV | Lower ABV = easier to balance |
| Solids% | 30-38% | Lower than non-boozy (alcohol replaces solids) |

### Key Rules
- **Alcohol dramatically affects freezing point** — must be calculated, not guessed
- **Low-ABV liqueurs (17% — Baileys, cream liqueurs)** are much easier to work with than 40% spirits
- **40% spirits (rum, whiskey):** Max ~50g per ~1kg batch or it won't freeze
- **Solids drop** when alcohol is added — may need extra SMP or inulin to compensate
- **Stevia drops** (3g) can boost sweetness without affecting freezing point — useful when alcohol already maxes out FPD

### Translation to Our Style
- **Same alcohol amounts** — booze doesn't need sugar-free swapping
- **Base compensation more critical** — alcohol + no sugar + low fat = triple hit on solids and FPD. Use more inulin (25-30g) + glycerin (15-20g) to compensate
- **Rum raisin:** Soak raisins in rum (same technique). Base swap as usual
- **Baileys:** Provides its own fat and sweetness. Reduce other sweeteners accordingly

## Keto / Sugar-Free

### Traditional Baseline (Nick's keto recipes)
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 12-19% | Still uses cream — not low-fat |
| Sweetener | 80-150g allulose or xylitol per ~1kg | Never erythritol alone |
| Inulin | 25-36g per ~1kg | For solids replacement |
| Protein powder | 43-85g per ~1kg | For body + protein content |
| Stabilizer | 1-2g (guar or tara) | |

### Key Insights from Nick's Keto Recipes
- **Nick hates erythritol for ice cream** — "it recrystallizes regardless, it's just a matter of time"
- **Xylitol preferred** over erythritol for keto ice cream
- **Allulose** is his go-to for sugar-free (we don't have it — our equivalent is erythritol + xylitol + glycerin)
- **Inulin** provides the solids that sugar/allulose would normally contribute
- **Protein powder** must be added OFF heat (40C max) — overheating makes it thick and gummy
- **Cocoa powder** still needs 55-60C to properly disperse even in "cold-process" recipes
- **Commercial protein ice cream is mostly air** — Halo Top is 269g vs Ben & Jerry's 423g for same container size. 25-40% overrun = quality. Above 40% = frozen dessert, not ice cream

### Translation to Our Style
- **Already closest to our recipes** — these are the recipes most directly applicable
- **Key difference:** Nick still uses cream (12-19% fat). We use 1.5% milk → need more body from whey, inulin, MCC
- **Allulose → our SweEX** (erythritol 60% + xylitol 40%). Use 80-100% of allulose weight. Add glycerin (10-15g) for extra FPD to compensate for erythritol's lower PAC vs allulose
- **Watch crystallization:** Keep erythritol under 8% of mix weight. If recipe needs more sweetener, shift ratio toward xylitol

## Sorbet

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Sugar% | 22-28% | MUST be high or sorbet is dry/powdery |
| Solids% | 25-35% | Lower than ice cream |
| Fruit | 200-500g per ~1kg batch | Primary ingredient |
| Stabilizer | 2-5g (guar, pectin, or tara) | Higher than ice cream |

### Key Rules
- **Sorbet needs 25-35% sweetener content** or it becomes "super dry and powdery" — this is non-negotiable
- **Corn syrup/invert sugar** changes serving temp dramatically (from -10C to -16C) — makes sorbet scoopable from freezer
- **Pectin** works as both stabilizer AND thickener in sorbet (5g per ~1kg)
- **Each fruit needs its own balance** — no "one recipe fits all." Mango, raspberry, strawberry all have different sugar/water content
- **Ninja Creami excels at sorbet** — better shearing than traditional churning → smoother, denser result

### Translation to Our Style
- **Sweetener content:** Replace sugar with xylitol (primary) + inulin (for solids). Nick's sugar-free hibiscus: 240g xylitol + 144g inulin for 1.5L = per pint: ~110g xylitol + ~65g inulin. That's over our 30g xylitol GI limit — must reduce and compensate with glycerin + erythritol
- **Realistic per-pint target:** Xylitol 25-30g + erythritol 30-40g + glycerin 15-20g + inulin 25-30g. Total "sweetener" ~100-120g for a 500g sorbet = 20-24% sweetener content. Below Nick's 25-35% target but Creami shearing compensates
- **Corn syrup substitute:** Glycerin (10-15g) provides similar FPD and texture effect
- **Stabilizer:** Guar gum 1-2g (cold-process). Tara gum if cooking fruit anyway (needs 75-85C)

## Cheesecake / Cream

### Traditional Baseline
| Parameter | Range | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Fat% | 14-22% | Mascarpone/cream cheese add significant fat |
| Sugar% | 14-18% | |
| Cheese | 150-250g per ~1kg batch | Mascarpone, cream cheese, or ricotta |
| SMP | 30-65g | |

### Key Ratios (per ~680g Creami pint)
- **Cream cheese:** 100-170g (our ADORO light cream cheese works)
- **Mascarpone:** 150-170g (40-45% fat — very rich)
- **Lemon zest:** 1 whole lemon for cheesecake brightness
- **Vanilla extract:** 5-10g

### Technique
- **Burnt Basque cheesecake as mix-in:** Bake cheesecake separately (600g cream cheese + 4 eggs + 225g sugar + 40g flour + 300g cream + vanilla), cut into chunks, fold into churned base. Lower oven to 180C fan (200C burns it)
- **Lemon rind infusion:** Zest into base for bright citrus without adding liquid
- **Philly-style base** (no eggs) works well for cheesecake — the cheese provides enough body
- **Graham cracker/biscuit crust pieces:** Bake separately, cool, cube. Don't use Creami Mix-In cycle for buttery crumbs

### Translation to Our Style
- **Our cream cheese (ADORO light, 11% fat, 148kcal):** Naturally lower fat than traditional — works perfectly for our style. Use 80-120g per pint
- **Cream cheese as body ingredient:** Cream cheese adds solids, protein, fat, and tang — partially replaces what cream normally does. Consider using 30-50g even in non-cheesecake recipes for body
- **Lemon:** Citric acid (1-2g) can substitute for lemon juice tartness. Lemon zest is the better flavor source
- **Sweetener:** Cheesecake flavors handle erythritol cooling effect better than vanilla — the tang and richness mask it

## Technique Library (Ingredient-Agnostic)

These processes work regardless of whether you use sugar or our sugar-free pantry:

### Cocoa Blooming
Disperse cocoa powder in hot liquid (50-55C+). Cold cocoa = gritty, dull flavor. Even in "cold-process" Creami recipes, microwave a small portion of the milk, bloom cocoa in it, then combine with the cold base.

### Fruit Preparation
- **Cook-down method:** Simmer fresh/frozen fruit on hob, crush, reduce until viscous. Removes excess water that would create ice crystals
- **Jam method:** Use jam/preserves instead of fresh fruit. Pre-concentrated, consistent sugar, no excess water. Check sugar content carefully
- **Pineapple jam:** Cook 250g fruit + sugar + 3g pectin. Watch for boiling stage — that's when water is stabilized. Skip this and you get icy lumps
- **Roasted banana:** 175C, 10-15 min, skins go black. Steep flesh in base overnight, strain before churning. Deep flavor without banana solids in final product

### Nut Paste
Blitz shelled nuts with sugar/sweetener + SMP in blender, then add small amounts of warmed milk (~100ml) to turn mixture into liquid paste. Do NOT use food processor (overheats, won't get smooth).

### Swirl / Ribbon
- **Chocolate fudge:** Melt condensed milk + dark chocolate + instant coffee. Cool to thick pourable consistency. Layer when packing into tub
- **Stracciatella:** 100g chocolate + 10-15g olive oil (or coconut oil). Melt, pour in thin stream into spinning ice cream → creates fine chocolate flakes that harden instantly
- **Caramel/DDL:** Warm to pourable consistency. Layer alternating with ice cream when packing. Don't use Creami Mix-In cycle (distributes too sparingly)
- **Fruit swirl:** Cook down fruit with sugar to thick sauce consistency. Cool completely before layering

### Mix-In Handling
- **Hard cookies (Oreos):** Break into desired chunk size before adding. Creami blade will break them down further
- **Buttery/crumbly items** (graham cracker, cookie dough): Do NOT use Creami Mix-In cycle — they disintegrate. Fold in by hand after spinning
- **Cookie dough cubes:** Heat-treat flour (175C, 10 min) for food safety. Chill dough, cut to ~1cm cubes
- **Nuts:** Toast first for deeper flavor. Freeze before adding to prevent sinking
- **Marshmallow:** Very sticky — warm slightly to swirl, or layer cold

### Coffee Infusion
- **Brewed espresso in base:** 60-80g per pint. Adds water — account in total liquid balance
- **Instant coffee powder:** 5-15g. Best for intensity without water impact. Doesn't affect freezing point
- **Coffee extract (alcohol-based):** 4-5g. Most concentrated option
- **Steeping whole beans:** Warm milk, add beans, steep 30+ min, strain. Loses ~10g — top up liquid

### Base Aging
4-24h in fridge before freezing. Essential for: stabilizer hydration, fat crystallization, protein hydration, flavor development (especially vanilla and malt). Nick considers 12-24h minimum for best results.

### Stracciatella / Chocolate Chip
100g chocolate + 10-15g oil (olive or coconut). Melt together. Pour in thin stream into churning/spinning ice cream. The cold ice cream hardens the chocolate instantly into delicate flakes/chips. Pour LOTS — don't be shy.
