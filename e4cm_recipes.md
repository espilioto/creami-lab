#### Chocolate Ice Cream #1 — Basic (Almond Milk + Cow's Milk Blend)
Tags: chocolate, low-calorie, simple
**Source:** Chocolate Ice Cream Made with MAXIMUM Flavor
**Base:**
- 1% milk — 160g (or 80g fat-free milk + 80g 2% milk to make your own 1%)
- unsweetened almond milk — 80g
- vanilla extract — 2.1g
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- erythritol or Swerve — 50g
- cocoa powder (King Arthur blend recommended) — 8g
- salt — 1g
- xanthan gum — 1g
**Steps:**
1. Add milk and almond milk and vanilla extract to the pint on a scale.
2. In a small bowl, combine erythritol, cocoa powder, salt, and xanthan gum.
3. Using a hand blender or immersion blender, mix the wet ingredients while slowly pouring in the dry ingredients (~30 seconds).
4. Continue mixing for an additional 90 seconds to fully dissolve and disperse evenly.
5. Put a lid on the pint, freeze for 24 hours.
6. Rinse outside under hot water for 1 minute, dry off, load up and run on Light Ice Cream setting.
7. After first spin, run again on Mix-In setting (for pint consistency) or Re-Spin (for soft serve consistency).
**Tips from this video:**
- Use high-protein milk (like Fairlife) for nearly double the protein at the same calories.
- Don't skip xanthan gum — it makes ice cream remarkably smoother and creamier.
- Use a high-quality cocoa powder (King Arthur blend recommended).

#### Chocolate Ice Cream #2 — All Cow's Milk (Improved Texture)
Tags: chocolate, improved-texture, low-calorie
**Source:** Chocolate Ice Cream Made with MAXIMUM Flavor
**Base:**
- 1% milk — 440g (strictly cow's milk)
- vanilla extract — 2.1g
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- Swerve — 50g
- cocoa powder — 8g
- salt — 1g
- xanthan gum — 1g
**Steps:**
1. Add 440g of 1% milk and vanilla extract to a high-sided container.
2. In a small bowl, combine Swerve, cocoa powder, salt, and xanthan gum. Mix to combine.
3. Use an immersion blender (recommended over hand blender) to blend the wet ingredients. Slowly pour in the dry ingredients.
4. Once dissolved, continue mixing for another 90 seconds.
5. Pour into pint, label, freeze for 24 hours.
6. Rinse outside, run on Light Ice Cream setting, then Mix-In or Re-Spin.
**Tips from this video:**
- Strictly cow's milk drastically improves texture and consistency. Almond milk is ~98% water vs milk at ~87% water.
- The protein composition found in milk leads to a drastic difference in ice crystal formation, creating better mouth feel and smoother texture.
- Immersion blender keeps cocoa powder suspended in the liquid (prevents sinking to bottom), leading to darker/richer ice cream and better final consistency.

#### Chocolate Ice Cream #3 — Ultimate (Egg Yolk + Melted Chocolate)
Tags: chocolate, premium, egg-yolk, melted-chocolate, best
**Source:** Chocolate Ice Cream Made with MAXIMUM Flavor
**Base:**
- 1% milk — 410g
- egg yolk — 1
- vanilla extract — 2.1g
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- Swerve — 50g
- cocoa powder — 10g
- salt — 1g
- xanthan gum — 1g
**Chocolate:**
- semisweet chocolate (baker's chocolate preferred, chocolate chips also work) — 14g
**Steps:**
1. Add 410g of 1% milk, egg yolk, and vanilla extract to a high-sided container.
2. In a small bowl, combine Swerve, cocoa powder, salt, and xanthan gum.
3. In another small bowl, add 14g semisweet chocolate.
4. Mix wet ingredients with immersion blender for 30 seconds.
5. Microwave the milk mixture in 30-second intervals until it reaches 80-90 degrees F. Mix with a spoon.
6. Melt the chocolate in the microwave for 45-60 seconds. Mix until even consistency.
7. Pour melted chocolate into the warm milk mixture. Blend for 30 seconds.
8. Slowly add dry ingredients until dissolved. Continue mixing for 90 seconds.
9. Optionally pour through a strainer into the pint (to catch any hardened chocolate or cooked egg).
10. Wait about 10 minutes before putting the lid on and putting it in the freezer.
11. Freeze for **36 hours** (not 24 — because the liquid is much hotter).
12. Rinse outside for 1 minute, run Light Ice Cream setting, then Mix-In setting for Haagen-Dazs-like consistency.
**Tips from this video:**
- Egg yolk adds a tremendous boost in taste and texture. Confirmed via blind taste test.
- If you don't want to use egg yolk, sub in another 15g of milk.
- Warm the milk to 80-90F before adding melted chocolate so it stays liquid — if added cold, chocolate hardens and becomes useless.
- Microwave in 30-second intervals, check temperature. Too hot = chunks of curdled milk and cooked egg.
- Wait 10 minutes before freezing — he does this for every ice cream now to avoid a frozen lump in the middle that could break the Creami.
- Freeze 36 hours for this recipe (hot liquid needs longer). At 24 hours you get frozen yogurt consistency.
- Make 4-5 pints at a time so you never have to wait.

#### Vanilla Ice Cream #1 — Basic (Almond Milk + Cow's Milk Blend)
Tags: vanilla, low-calorie, budget-friendly
**Source:** Ice Cream is Great for Weight Loss
**Base:**
- 1% milk (Fairlife recommended: 120g fat-free + 120g 2%) — 240g
- unsweetened almond milk — 200g
- vanilla extract — 5g
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- Swerve (or erythritol) — 45g
- xanthan gum — 1g
- salt — 1g
**Steps:**
1. Add milk, almond milk, and vanilla extract to a pint on a scale.
2. In a small bowl, combine Swerve, xanthan gum, and salt. Mix with a spoon or fork.
3. Using a frother or immersion blender, start mixing the wet ingredients in the pint. While mixing, slowly add the dry ingredients.
4. Once all dry ingredients are poured, continue mixing for an additional 90 seconds.
5. Cap the pint and freeze for 24 hours. Label with masking tape + Sharpie.
6. Rinse outside under hot water for 1 minute, dry off, load up and hit Ice Cream function.
7. After first spin, re-spin for ice cream consistency, or re-spin one more time for soft serve.
**Tips from this video:**
- Lowest calorie and most cost-effective option.
- Adding dry ingredients while actively mixing prevents xanthan gum from clumping.
- Ice Cream function worked best in his testing (double-blind trial in his kitchen). But texture preference is individual.

#### Vanilla Bean Ice Cream #3 — Ultimate (Egg Yolk + Vanilla Bean)
Tags: vanilla-bean, premium, egg-yolk, best
**Source:** Ice Cream is Great for Weight Loss
**Base:**
- 2% milk — 430g
- egg yolk — 1
- vanilla extract — 5g
- vanilla bean — 1/2 (seeds scraped out)
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- Swerve — 45g
- xanthan gum — 1g
- salt — 1g
**Steps:**
1. Add 430g of 2% milk, egg yolk, and 5g vanilla extract to a high-sided container on a scale.
2. Cut a vanilla bean in half, open it up, scrape out half the seeds with a knife. Drop seeds into the wet ingredients. Store other half in airtight container.
3. In a small bowl, combine Swerve, xanthan gum, and salt.
4. Using an immersion blender (highly recommended for this recipe), start blending the wet ingredients while slowly pouring in the dry ingredients.
5. Mix for an additional 90 seconds. Dump the liquid into a pint.
6. Freeze for 24 hours.
7. Rinse under hot water 60 seconds, load into Creami, run Ice Cream setting. Repeat re-spin for desired consistency.
**Tips from this video:**
- Egg yolk acts as an emulsifier — helps bind different liquids together for smoother, creamier consistency. Confirmed via blind taste test.
- If you don't want to use egg yolk, add 15 extra grams of milk.
- Vanilla beans make a massive difference in overall flavor. Get good quality ones.
- Immersion blender not only incorporates xanthan gum more easily but also thickens the mixture by emulsifying it more than the frother ever could.
- If using a frother (no immersion blender), most vanilla beans will sink to the bottom. Workaround: take the pint out of freezer after about 1 hour and swirl the mixture around for 10-15 seconds.
- Don't throw away empty vanilla pods — you can make homemade vanilla extract from them.
- Best investment: buying extra pints so you always have ice cream ready without waiting 24 hours.

#### Cinnamon Toast Crunch Ice Cream — Cereal Edition 1
Tags: cereal, cinnamon-toast-crunch, low-calorie
**Source:** 150 Calorie Pints of Ice Cream | Cereal Edition | 3 Ways
**Base (pint):**
- 1% Fairlife milk (or combine fat-free + 2% to make 1%) — 160g
- unsweetened almond milk — 280g
- vanilla extract — 4.2g
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- pure cane (erythritol) — 50g
- cinnamon — 1.5g
- salt — 1g
- xanthan gum — 1g
**Mix-in:**
- Cinnamon Toast Crunch cereal (or generic version) — 10g
**Steps:**
1. Add milk, almond milk, and vanilla extract to the pint on a scale.
2. In a separate bowl, add pure cane, cinnamon, salt, and xanthan gum. Mix dry ingredients with a spoon, fork, or whisk so they dissolve easier.
3. Using a hand blender, start mixing wet ingredients while slowly pouring in the dry ingredients. Mix as slow as possible to avoid creating too much foam (which causes overflow), but fast enough to combine everything without clumping. Keep the mixer all the way at the bottom of the pint to prevent foaming.
4. After all dry ingredients are mixed in, blend for another 90 seconds to ensure everything is dissolved.
5. Put the top on the pint and freeze for 24 hours.
6. Run the pint under warm water for 40 seconds. Dry off, pop the top off, lock into the Creami. Run Ice Cream setting.
7. After first spin, re-spin. Then use a butter knife to make a hole down the middle.
8. Fill the hole with 10g Cinnamon Toast Crunch cereal. Load back up and use the Mix-In setting.
**Tips from this video:**
- Xanthan gum is the most important ingredient — it helps keep ice crystals from forming, especially when using almond milk (which is essentially water).
- A bag of xanthan gum will last you a year or more.
- Having 1g vs 1.7g of xanthan gum can completely change your finished product — use an accurate scale (0.1g resolution recommended).
- To prevent overflow, you can follow the same steps in a taller and wider vessel instead of directly in the pint, then transfer over.
- Don't mix on high speed even in a larger vessel — the froth still causes spillage when you transfer to the pint.
- Real vanilla extract (not imitation) is crucial for ice cream since it is not heated up.
- Generic cereal tastes the same and is way cheaper.

#### Watermelon Sorbet
Tags: sorbet, watermelon, dairy-free, low-calorie, 3-ingredient
**Source:** Sorbet Recipe 3 Ways (3 Ingredients)
**Ingredients (pint):**
- fresh watermelon juice (blended/pureed) — 330g
- water — 120g
- erythritol — 20g
- salt — 0.5g
**Steps:**
1. Cut watermelon into chunks, blend/puree to make juice.
2. Add 330g watermelon juice to the pint on a scale. Add 120g water, 20g erythritol, and 0.5g salt.
3. Mix with an immersion blender or hand blender for about 30 seconds until combined.
4. Put a lid on the pint and freeze for 24 hours.
5. Run the pint under lukewarm/hot water for 60 seconds. Lock into place and hit the Sorbet button.
6. After first spin, use the back of a spoon to smooth and level everything out in the middle. Re-spin.
7. Re-spin one more time (total: Sorbet + 2 re-spins) for real sorbet consistency.
**Tips from this video:**
- Watermelon juice will separate after blending — stir it before pouring into the pint.
- Taste the mixture before freezing — it should be sweeter than you want because freezing dulls the sweetness.
- Salt helps bring out the sweetness of the fruit.
- One watermelon can make about 15 pints at roughly $4 total.
- This is only 3 ingredients (fruit, sweetener, salt) plus water.
- Level everything out with a spoon between re-spins for even consistency.

#### Cherry Garcia Protein Ice Cream
Tags: cherry-garcia, protein, egg-yolk, mix-ins, premium
**Source:** Cherry Garcia Protein Ice Cream Creami Recipe
**Base (pint):**
- bing cherry juice (from the jar) — 20g
- 2% high protein milk (Fairlife) — 380g
- egg yolk — 2
- vanilla extract — 4g
**Dry mix (small bowl):**
- vanilla whey/casein blend protein powder (PEScience preferred) — 16g
- Swerve — 40g
- salt — 0.5g
- xanthan gum — 0.8g
**Mix-ins:**
- bing cherries — 5 (stems removed, opened, pressed into triple-layer paper towels to remove juice)
- Hershey's dark chocolate — 15g (chopped into 1/8 inch chunks)
**Steps:**
1. Add 20g bing cherry juice, 380g 2% high protein milk, 2 egg yolks, and 4g vanilla extract to a high-sided container.
2. In a small bowl, combine 16g protein powder, 40g Swerve, 0.5g salt, and 0.8g xanthan gum. Mix dry ingredients together.
3. Using an immersion blender, mix the wet ingredients while slowly adding the dry ingredients over about 30 seconds.
4. Continue blending for an additional 90 seconds until everything is fully dissolved.
5. Pour into the pint. Freeze for 24 hours.
6. Prepare mix-ins: remove stems from 5 bing cherries, open them, and press into triple-layer paper towels to remove as much juice as possible. Chop 15g Hershey's dark chocolate into 1/8 inch chunks.
7. Run pint under hot water for 1 minute. Lock into Creami and run on Sorbet setting (for thickest consistency like Ben & Jerry's).
8. Load mix-ins: alternate layers of chocolate chunks and cherries in the pint.
9. Run Mix-In setting.
**Tips from this video:**
- Originally a vanilla base, but switching to cherry base with bing cherry juice transforms the flavor.
- Use bing cherries — they are the same type Ben & Jerry's uses. Rainer cherries do not have the same taste.
- You MUST remove the cherry juice by pressing into paper towels — if the juice gets into the ice cream, it melts and crystallizes it.
- Swerve makes smoother ice cream than sucralose.
- If you don't want egg yolks, substitute 30g of 2% milk — but it drastically reduces flavor.

# TIPS & TECHNIQUES

*Compiled primarily from "3 Years of CREAMi Advice in 25 Minutes" (video 011) plus tips gathered across all recipe videos.*

## Machine & Settings

1. **Some Creami settings are basically clones with different names.** After extensive testing, the regular Ice Cream setting actually came out on top — not Light Ice Cream. Test settings individually for each recipe to find the best one.

2. **Different Creami models produce different results.** Ninja quietly tweaked the programs between model releases. The same setting on a Deluxe is slightly different from the original (OG) model, and the Swirl is very different. For 1:1 accuracy with E4CM recipes, the original model is recommended.

3. **The Swirl model is not recommended.** They tweaked many settings compared to the original. Most E4CM recipes will not come out right on a Swirl. It costs double the money, takes up twice the space, and the CREAMi Fit function makes cold, airy foam instead of ice cream. Unless you specifically want swirl cones, skip it.

4. **The machine is loud — and that is okay.** It is literally shaving down a block of ice. Expecting it to be quiet does not make sense on a technical level. That is just how the physics work.

5. **The machine is more durable than you think.** Most people who broke their machines froze essentially plain water, which turns into a brick of cement. A machine can do 1,000+ pints over 3 years and still work perfectly. Ninja's warranty is solid, and Costco returns are even easier.

6. **Buy smart.** The CREAMi's $229 retail price can be steep. Warehouse stores like Costco drop them to $99 on sale multiple times a year. Facebook Marketplace is another source for the same deal.

7. **The machine is just the down payment.** If you want to do it right from your first recipe, extra pints, bulk ingredients, etc. — expect to spend an additional $100 to $200 on top of the machine.

## Ingredients — Milk & Dairy

8. **Use high protein milk (Fairlife).** It has double the protein for the same calories, making smoother ice cream thanks to additional casein. It lasts longer in the fridge and has less water content, leading to better (fewer) ice crystal formation.

9. **Almond milk is not really milk — it is nut water.** Compared to regular milk, it has about 10% more water, which means more ice crystals and less creaminess. However, mixed with real dairy in the right ratio it can produce an epically low calorie pint when calories are tight.

10. **1% milk works excellently as a base for most recipes.** There is never a hard rule for every recipe, but more times than not, excellent ice cream can be made with 1% milk — just include the other key ingredients like xanthan gum and salt.

11. **Do not strip out every gram of fat.** Many other creators' recipes taste lifeless because fat equals flavor. Adding a little fat gives body and creaminess that is worth the extra 40 to 100 calories per pint.

12. **Egg yolks are the secret to gelato-level creaminess.** Gelato makers cut down on cream so they could load up with yolks, which is an emulsifier that drastically increases creaminess. Anytime you have the calories to spare, add a yolk. Companies like Halo Top and Enlightened have unlimited money to make their ice cream taste great, but with the right ingredients and a Creami, you can beat them in your own kitchen.

## Ingredients — Sweeteners

13. **Erythritol is the best zero-calorie sweetener for ice cream.** After testing them all, erythritol won — it is the only sweetener with no FDA daily intake limit, causes the least digestive issues even in large amounts, and has the most sugar-like taste with the least aftertaste. Studies about clotting/heart disease were badly misrepresented.

14. **A small amount of real sugar makes a big difference.** Most recipes are fine with only zero-calorie sweeteners, but some need a small boost — just 10g (about 40 calories) of real sugar — to smooth out aftertaste, cut ice crystals, and make it taste like legit ice cream.

15. **Frozen foods taste less sweet than at room temperature.** That is the exact reason why single-ingredient ice creams like chocolate milk or protein shakes make no sense — chocolate milk tastes perfect from the fridge, but freeze it and spin it and the chocolate flavor goes weak and the sweetness disappears.

16. **Every ingredient drastically changes the recipe, including different versions of the same ingredient.** Different sweeteners change freezing point and texture differently.

## Ingredients — Protein & Stabilizers

17. **Protein powder does more than just boost protein.** It improves creaminess, texture, and reduces iciness. But if it does not taste fantastic on its own, your pint is doomed before it even spins.

18. **Whey/casein blends are too good at thickening.** Using more than about half a scoop makes ice cream like frozen cookie dough. That is why most E4CM recipes use mostly whey protein isolate.

19. **Too much protein powder turns ice cream into a glorified protein shake.** More is not always better — the goal is ice cream that tastes like ice cream.

20. **Xanthan gum is the best stabilizer for Creami ice cream.** It does far more than just thicken — it holds water, smooths texture, and gives a fatty mouthfeel without fat. It won over all other stabilizers in blind taste tests.

21. **Salt belongs in every single recipe.** It does not matter if it is a hot recipe, cold recipe, sweet recipe, or savory recipe. Salt enhances every flavor compound and makes your pint taste complete. Do not skip it.

## Ingredients — Extracts & Flavorings

22. **Use real vanilla extract, not imitation.** Real vanilla extract has over 200 flavor and aroma compounds; imitation vanilla has just one. The extract is worth every penny. Save the imitation vanilla for baking recipes like blueberry muffins.

23. **All extracts are different — how much you need depends heavily on the brand.** One brand's peppermint extract at 12 drops could be another brand's at 4 drops. Start small and adjust.

24. **Some extracts can mask bad-tasting protein powder.** Peppermint extract in particular can cover up some of the worst-tasting protein powders ever created.

25. **Pudding mix is not ideal.** While it can work in a pinch, it just puts more artificial flavoring in your ice cream, taking away from your pint tasting like real ice cream.

26. **Cottage cheese ice cream recipes taste terrible.** Any cottage cheese recipe you see online is not worth making.

## Ingredients — Chocolate & Mix-ins

27. **Frozen chocolate chips taste like crayons.** The fix: melt chocolate and mix with a touch of oil (peanut oil) before freezing, then chop into pieces for mix-ins.

28. **The Mix-In setting obliterates mix-ins.** It crushes them into crumbs instead of gently folding them in. Various methods were developed to keep mix-ins intact — for example, milk-dunked frozen Oreos, or placing mix-ins in a hole near the side of the pint.

29. **Speed is everything when adding mix-ins.** Once the hole is made, get the mix-in into the pint and back to spinning ASAP. Otherwise, the ice cream melts and the mix-in gets pulverized.

30. **Make the hole off-center or in the corner of the pint** so the blade does less damage to the mix-in.

31. **Some mix-ins are just too soft to survive the blade.** For certain ones (like lemon bar pieces), the trick is freezing them first — keep them frozen, cut the hole, then drop them in right before the mixing cycle.

32. **Low-calorie mix-ins have their place.** They are easy to make in bulk, last for months, and increase the protein in your pint — like the cookie dough recipe that makes enough for six pints.

## Technique — Mixing & Blending

33. **Use an immersion blender, not a frother.** A frother makes liquid foamy (bad) and has weak mixing power. An immersion blender fully hydrates dry ingredients, dissolves stabilizers, and evenly distributes everything.

34. **Even when dry ingredients look dissolved, they are not fully integrated.** Give it another 90 seconds of blending so everything fully dissolves and disperses evenly.

35. **Use a kitchen scale for all measurements.** Small additions like 0.5g xanthan gum need to be weighed precisely, or you can end up with gummy bear-textured ice cream.

## Technique — Freezing

36. **Some recipes need longer than 24 hours to freeze.** When the milk gets heated (like in Death by Chocolate), the pint can take a full 36 hours to reach freezing point. If a pint is already melted after just one spin, it was not frozen long enough.

37. **The frozen hump in the middle can damage your machine.** Two fixes: (1) Let foam rise and liquid settle for 15 minutes, then freeze with the cap off. (2) Put pint in the fridge for 3-4 hours first, then freeze with the cap off. If a hump still appears, rub the top with a hot damp paper towel before spinning.

38. **Foam going over the max fill line is not dangerous.** Foam floats to the top when freezing and pushes down easily with your fingers — it will not damage the machine.

## Technique — Spinning & Finishing

39. **When ice cream is 90-95% there but not quite smooth, run the Mix-In setting without any mix-ins.** One empty spin on Mix-In can push it over the edge into perfectly smooth cream.

40. **Icy sides of the pint cannot be fully fixed** (a bigger spindle would scrape plastic). But rinsing the outside with hot water for 1 minute before spinning, then holding the pint in your hands as you eat, melts most of the icy texture away.

41. **Re-spinning leftovers never recaptures the original quality.** After months of experimentation, re-frozen and re-spun pints never achieve the same creaminess and mouthfeel as when the pint was first spun.

## Maintenance & Care

42. **Clean the Creami lid after every use — it grows mold.** Leftover ice cream gets trapped inside the lid and slowly ferments. Clean every single part (lid, paddle, spinner) after each use.

44. **Some homemade recipes stand toe-to-toe with full-fat brands** like Ben & Jerry's — at half the calories and triple the protein.
